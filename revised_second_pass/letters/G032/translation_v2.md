## Revised English Translation

Dear charm of speech, helper against sickness, how sweetly you came to me at the proper moment. These are Orestes' words, as you know, my dearest head; I parody them slightly and speak them of you. Shall I add the words of the comic poet too: how glad I am, how delighted, how I wish to dance?

But why do I anoint your little letter with the oil of sinners, when it can be brightened with the gifts of the cultivated olive? "My soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit rejoiced in God," because he clothed me with a garment of gladness and a robe of glory instead of a spirit of despondency. He tore my sackcloth and girded me with joy, because he gave me the backs of my enemies, destroyed those who hate me, and sent a man before us to heal the broken, visit the sick, and become all things to all for salvation.

Excellent, then, that you appeared in our regions as a warder-off of evils. These matters had already been given up for lost, and even Lysias would have said that the wickedness of rulers had finished its work. First the castle-builder was stripping the pitiful people bare; now the katepano was winnowing even the dust. There was also some third man among them, akin to Pharaoh at least by name. I do not know whether he is in any way more tolerable than those already mentioned.

But now they have been visited by a visitation from on high, and reason will stand over them. If I have not yet forgotten my Nicholas, dear to me in every way, they will rejoice in return for the days and years in which they were humbled and in which we saw evils. This, then, will be theirs, and I am a messenger announcing good news of what is to come.

As for Constantine Choirosphaktes, whom we have sent ahead from our side, in whatever matters he needs you for the Church's poor little property, let him be saved through your protection. Being otherwise rather simple and soft, unless he receives much support from you, he may appear entirely unapproved. May the Lord keep you above every evil and injury.
