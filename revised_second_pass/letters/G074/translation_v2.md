## Revised English Translation

"I would wish it, and it would be far better," most holy head, if those who injure us were restrained toward something more humane by the deaths of others. But now they are so tireless in wickedness that, like that Egyptian, they are rather hardened by blows. Nor would this be manly if they were hardened only by the wounds of their own people; already they are hardened even by their own wounds. They do what many wild beasts are said to do when wounded: they spare themselves still less and leap more passionately into their wounds, so that, since they are going to suffer, they may also do something to those who fight them.

If you want, those who recount the story of Herod son of Antipater say that he planned an achievement at the point of death worthy of his savagery. He gathered all the Jewish notables into the courtyard of the palace to have them slaughtered together like sacrificial victims, saying, "So no Jewish house, occupied with mourning, will have leisure to applaud my death."

Such, then, seem to me the calculations of the beastly Herodians of the present days. When struck they are provoked further; when dying they display their evil alive and immortal. The nearer they foresee divine destruction coming, the more they hasten to destroy others along with themselves, so that those who depart before them may not see their destruction, laugh at it, or have any time at all to relax - those whom they wished always to mourn and look gloomy.

If, then, you hope to change the will of one of the demons, whether persuaded or forced by the dispensations of God's goodness, hope also that our executioners may be made good by the sterner proceedings of manifold wisdom. But if you do not hope the former, I do not see how you can suppose the latter. For in these men too wickedness, fixed into habit, has taken on the force of nature; I am persuaded of this by what they do.

Nevertheless we must do what is owed: always pray for their correction. The Lord too, testing the reins of Judas, knew that he was incorrigible, yet did not cease showing the works of goodness. Do not cease, then, both to intercede on their behalf yourself and to exhort others, as many as you know to be strong, to the same work, in case beyond hope, by many hands, we may move the stone.
