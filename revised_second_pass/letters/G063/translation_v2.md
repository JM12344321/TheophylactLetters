## Revised English Translation

...as something that could have happened did not happen. Since neither of the two alternatives is admissible, it is truly foolish to seek what is impossible. Let us therefore choose the other: I mean that, when that man has been taken from us, we ourselves should enjoy some relief.

But that would belong to people who falsify their love for him, who love themselves rather than their brother, and who are so far from the commandment that orders one to love one's neighbor more than oneself that they do not even fulfill the measure of legal love, which assigns equality to neighbors in relation to ourselves. And this is in the case of one who loved us more than himself and showed it by the things he endured, spending himself so that we might pass our life in ease.

Besides, how is it not absurd to cling to that man because he had loyalty toward us, for which he exposed himself to every danger, while we ourselves do not trust the common Master or entrust ourselves confidently to him for the course of life that remains to us, as though it would be lighter with God and have nothing rough or steep? The one who cares for us is the same one who then cared for us through him. He is the same and has not changed. He will shepherd us, and nothing will be lacking to us.

When Moses came to rest, did Israel remain without a guardian? Did it not have Joshua as guide and general, the type of the greater one, minister of greater things, and fulfiller of the promise concerning the desired land, of which Moses was not deemed worthy? When Elijah was wondrously snatched up, did Elisha not appear as a prophet with a double spirit? I believe that from now on our affairs will be better than before, so that we may learn that we are carried through, beyond any human conception, not by human devices and efforts but by divine goodness and words of power.

It is fitting that, reflecting on these things and more than these, you should rise above every faintness of soul and lowness of thought. When they came into my mind, they allowed me too to recover from the collapse caused by what had happened. Hold yourself so, most honored brother, and do not let the arrows of children shake the tower of your understanding, children who play games with the very things in which they busy themselves against us.

For you have already founded that tower on the rock, as a wise man; and the rock is Christ. The winds will blow and the rivers will rush against it, from rain and permission from above. But may it remain firm and unshaken for you, so that you may boast in the Lord and say: "Our soul passed through the torrent; yes, our soul passed through the unstable water." And may the God of all consolation himself console you more perfectly through the Paraclete, the Spirit equal in power.

## Source And Revision Notes

- Gautier states that the beginning of this letter is mutilated because of missing folios.
- The Greek term for the loss of the protector is ambiguous; Gautier notes that it may denote disgrace/removal or death, though later `ἐκείνου` favors death.
- Biblical allusions include Luke 10:27, Malachi 3:6, Psalm 22/23:1, Matthew 7:24-25, Psalm 123/124:4-5, and 2 Corinthians 1:3.
