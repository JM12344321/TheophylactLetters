## Revised English Translation

My holy lord: you have been sent by God as a liberating god to the barbarian inhabited world where we live. To all who draw near to your God-crowned power you are consolation and refreshment; but especially to the weakness of my lowliness you are a rod of strength and a tower of power against the face of the enemy, whether this enemy should be understood as some unjust tempter or as grief within me driving in its arrows. If anything whatever troubles me, the ally is immediately near, and the pain-dispelling medicine is not far away.

For as soon as I remember your majesty, I am gladdened. Indeed, even now, while writing to your God-inspired power, I hope that release from every sorrow will come from the very act of writing. But these things have already been said. Such matters are scattered by the name of my mighty lord as the morning frost is scattered by the sun. Who, hearing that the God-crowned lord gladly receives letters from the archbishop and gives orders on behalf of those who receive mercy from him, will show anything harsh against me or mine?

Since it is the season of fasting and of eating fish, your guardian, the Theotokos, sends your majesty a blessing: two hundred little salted fish. She is poor in fish because of the loss of the strougai, the fish-channels, but rich in the protection which she always asks for your majesty.

But consider the number itself too as a symbol of more divine admonition and counsel. It teaches that the more perfect fivefold order of the senses, joined in many ways to the fourfold order of the virtues, should be offered as food and drink to the King of all by an unworthy servant.

## Consequential Changes From First Pass
