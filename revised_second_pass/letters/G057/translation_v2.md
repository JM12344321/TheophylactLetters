## Revised English Translation

The things that have happened to you, most honored brother, are grievous and touch every heart. Who will deny it? Fightings without, fears within; help and ally nowhere. The enemies make their noise and lift up their head; they make the bodies of servants food for the birds of the sky. They pour out their blood around the city in our charge, and there is no one to bury them. Friends and neighbors stand opposite; and if they draw near, they stand there preparing the deadly draught still more terribly. Since love has wholly grown cold, a man's enemies are those of his own house.

These things can sting the soul beyond words. Yet if we consider that every head is in pain and every heart in grief, the very universality of the disaster will become a consolation to us. Tell me, who has relief from these evil days? I think no one. Even if someone were so blessed, impossible as it is, as to have no private grief of his own, still, if he lives according to the laws of Christ, he will make the mourning of others his own, having received the command to weep with those who weep. If he is insensible to the common and brotherly misfortunes, he should rather be mourned as more wretched than all, even than those who are punished with the uttermost penalties. For if blessed are those who mourn and woe to those who laugh, consider what end we shall assign to such a man.

Do not, then, sink into faint-heartedness as though you alone were suffering terribly. You have bitter tax-collectors? But not more bitter than those in these parts, who take one of five children into slavery, just as they would take a fifth or a tenth from a herd of animals. You have Cumans driving in upon you from outside? What are they compared with the Achridians who descend on us from the city? They, treating the very act of going up into the city as a weapon of favor and a garland, carry everything off and sweep everything away, with no one to ransom or save. Who is there to resist the citizens?

You have bad men of Kastron? They are children compared with our Bulgarian men of Kastron. Or rather, so that I may not shame the greatness of the evil in our Kastron, what are your utterly wicked men compared with our rustic Mokrenoi? I have shifted these examples into the circumstances of Achris, which you too know, since you lived for no little time as a stranger in this valley of weeping, so that by one part I may show you the whole.
