## Revised English Translation

However troublesome I may seem to those who do not rightly cut through affairs with the sword of reason, I will not seem so to my lord, whose mind is greater. For any high priest who would blame a man for interceding on behalf of the burdens laid upon the people has surely pitched his tent far away from both mind and reason.

Therefore I shall speak and write for those entrusted to me, and I shall beseech from the very heart, as they say, the one who is able to have mercy. For Achrida, my most venerable protector, if it is not cherished by your mighty hand, will disappear from the middle of Bulgaria sooner than one could hope. It is small, utterly poor, suffering from scarcity of people, the most pitiable of all themes.

Let it therefore be pitied by your merciful and administrative authority in regard to the levy of infantrymen, and let the number be reduced. How will those levied from here not mark a conspicuous diminution upon the whole theme? How will even those who remain not spit upon the dear soil of their fatherland as hostile and disaster-making, and embrace the bosom of another land?

I beg, then, and I do not beg alone. I bring two fellow-ambassadors: justice itself, which the smallness of the theme puts forward, and the fact that it lies as a passageway and is useful for the safety of all travelers. Even if not for its own sake, then because it saves those passing through while itself being saved, it deserves mercy, especially from my lord.

The infantrymen already levied are enough to crush us. Let the levy go no further, through your very great protection, my lord who has come among us for our salvation. For, as I said, those already levied have sufficiently brought about the reduction of this little theme. Our territory is not Pelagonia; it is the proverbial tiny Mykonos.

May the Theotokos, to whom we belong and to whom you give everything that you give to us, grant you an unimpeded relation toward God and an unconquered stance toward enemies, so that you may be greater than all in both divine and human matters.
