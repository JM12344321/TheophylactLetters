## Revised English Translation

Glabenitzes goes from home to home, from us to you, brother honored by God, for we persuaded him too. In other respects the man is despondent: unwillingly, as it were, he has taken up the burden of the episcopate, and he retreats from the impossible weight of the matter as much as others seize it beforehand.

We treated the shield of his despondency with other verbal charms, and especially by chanting over him the hopes placed in you, and in this way we completed the whole matter. See, then, that you become for him what he was led to hope, you who are in every way noble and good. Let the treasure not turn out to be coals for him, as the saying goes; and let me not be accused either of foolishness or of malice: of foolishness, if I was deceived and never understood the man whom I insisted I knew better than I know myself; of malice, if I deceived the most well-disposed of bishops.

But let these worries go to the mountain or to the wave of the much-roaring sea. You, Heracles of virtue, clean out for our brother the unpleasant things in the dung-heap, and make sweeter the other matters that lie upon your knees. May it be through you that he leads golden days and does not grow gloomy because of some unwanted encounter.

For now, we ask you for a favor. Tomorrow you yourself will acknowledge that we stirred you up to make the good man better. May Christ, the first and great archbishop, in his goodness keep you above every evil and injury.
