## Revised English Translation

Who among us is more blessed now, when we have been richly blessed by a master's letter, when mind, eyes, tongue, and hearing have been sanctified and purified as we have understood, read through, and heard what was written? We had been in a bad and very precarious state, all-holy master, overcome by the present evil days and swallowed up by the deep waters. But the hand of your letter saved me from faint-heartedness and from the storm, raised me from the abyss, and drew me up from that mud and slime, imitating your Jesus toward the disciple who was sinking.

For you admonished us with that sweet tongue not to be disturbed by what happens, but to stand unbending and unchanged, and to keep the steadiness required amid unstable things. The admonition was lofty and tonic; the discourse dedicated itself to consolation; and, bringing with it divine power, it touched the heart gently, set it burning like that of Cleopas, and showed the exhortation to be active and effective.

What, compared with this little letter of yours, are long discourses with many lines, and wisdom that rings round the ears with empty noises? They are births of air resembling their father and flowing away with what flows. Do not cease, then, to help us in this way, watering a soul dry with drought, which ox and donkey tread down today - whatever exactly these may have to mean. I hesitate to illuminate for myself the covering of that dark saying.

But I had almost let escape the blessing of what you sent, and this too is a great admonition, one hardly commensurate with my folly. To the things called "anointers," equal in number to the presbyters of John's Apocalypse, I adapt the chrism with which we have been anointed from the Holy One. For this reason we are required more than others to present ourselves to the Lord and to bring prayers as incense, standing before God not only for our own sins but also for the ignorances of the people.

And what of the object that is round in surface, but in length rod-like and straight? Does not the one shape admonish me not to be wholly of the earth, but to touch it only in a small part, just as certain holy stones roll among the prophets? And does not the other signify the uprightness seen by the face of the Lord, by which those who possess it are made good, and also the pastoral and supporting office by which the high priest is known? And with the other four fragrant woods, what else am I instructed to do, beneath the depth of grace in you, but take up my cross and put to death my members upon the earth?

But we are being acted upon. The cross is the symbol of these operations of ours, both a nailing down and so called. These, then, are the meanings I imagined in the things you sent. If, as is likely, you meant something higher, something beyond and brighter than my weak sight, uncover my eyes, eyewitness and servant of the Word, so that you may benefit me more completely by enabling me to understand the marvels you possess, praying together with our wretchedness that there may be for us, after harsh days, a state of forgiveness and calm.

## Source And Revision Notes

- The second half interprets symbolic gifts sent by the patriarch, apparently chrism, a staff-like object, and fragrant woods.
- The phrase about "ox and donkey" is retained as allusive and uncertain; Theophylact himself says he hesitates to explain it.
