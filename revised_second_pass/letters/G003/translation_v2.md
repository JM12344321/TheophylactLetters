## Revised English Translation

Small is the gift of speech I offer to you, the great one, man of God. It is small because I have no leisure to let my tongue range over your good qualities in a more sophistic way, although I very much wished to give my tongue time for your praises and for the offspring of your auspicious tongue, especially now that you yourself have anticipated me and supplied the subject.

Such is a good soul, one that lives for God alone, looks toward the good, always serves the Word, and makes its study and concern the very thing you have now shown toward us, holy father and master.

For when you perceived that we were grieving and thought ourselves wronged, you did not wait for someone else to become your teacher of what is good and just. You anticipated him yourself and seized the good work, self-summoned, self-moved, needing no goad except that you were moved by God, for whom you promised to live and conduct yourself and toward whom you chose to walk.

We were grieving because we had been wronged. We grieved only a little, for what happened concerning us did not truly touch the very extremity of our heart. Yet it did touch us, because while the others were eating even the fat of wheat and enjoying abundant food, we, as though we were no part of the Church and had no ability to contribute something toward the common benefit, were receiving a small and inedible grain allowance. One might very aptly have said that line of David: "The loaves for the rhetor were both diminished and made bad."

For this reason I went about mourning and gloomy. I became deaf and was humbled, and I did not even come into your sight, avoiding both giving offense and taking offense. But you, man moved by God, instrument of the Spirit, who move and sing like a true ten-stringed psaltery, since you have finely tuned the five senses of the body with the five of the soul; you, like a drum made of skin because of mortification, struck and sounded together with the wood of the cross: you healed the pain and, like a physician, set a dressing on my wound. So, if I do not seem too bold, I shall attach to you even the words borne witness to God: "You made me suffer and then restored me; you struck, and your hands healed again."

And yet even if you had done toward us what you did while moved by another, even so you would have been worthy to be crowned with countless crowns. It is welcome when someone can be persuaded even by others, and all the wise, both ancient and recent, place this too among praiseworthy things. But as it is, without waiting for someone to tell you or remind you, you discerned for yourself what was fitting, and you did not even leave us a mere auspicious word. By doing a deed higher than the present age and the judgment of human beings, you confirm the monk in yourself and show that you walk after your brother's and master's footprints.

What footprints are these? Those of the shepherd and our protector. I can say of you both without fearing anyone: "Moses and Aaron among his priests." For Moses, who converses with God and from there draws the Law and the commandments by which he guides the new Israel, the fullness of the Church, leads us by a pillar of cloud and fire, even if we do not wish it. I mean by the light of reason and the lamp of discernment. To philosophize about this more plainly for you: the nature of fire is double, illuminating and burning; and the activity of discernment, as the wise would say, is double too. It benefits those who have made themselves worthy of such things, and it assigns the owed rank to those worthy of a lower and lesser one.

In this sense God is also called and believed by Moses to be fire. For this reason the high priest of old had scarlet woven into his robe, indicating the color and activity of fire, since the high priest must both illumine and burn in whatever directions reason moves him. Such, then, is his pillar of fire.

What is the cloud? It is the spiritual life, which provides us cooling from the passions. These truly burn our heads and the ruling part of the soul from above. This is also how I understand the prophetic promise, "By day the sun will not burn you, nor the moon by night." The sun is Satan, who deceives us as an angel of light; the moon is night and destructive darkness.

Such for us is Moses sent from God. But you, my master, are Aaron: yourself sanctified by the anointing of priesthood, second after him and first of the others after the first, second to Moses but foremost of the council, and drawing near to the God who draws near. How? By benefactions and by rubbing no evil onto anyone. For, as you know no less than anyone, most holy master, nothing makes a human being like God so much as showing beneficence. "The Lord is good to all"; "the Lord is compassionate"; "our God shows mercy"; God's mercy is upon all; and "be merciful, so that you may be like your Father in the heavens."

Since you have what makes a human being like the Lord, you draw near to God and have appeared Godlike. But broaden still the cords of your mercy and do not spare. Show your right hand without narrowness toward all, especially toward those who labor at good works useful to all, and still more if they have no help for life from anywhere. Toward our master, the common father, become still more kind to those who need reconciliation with him, since Aaron also served the Israelites and in turn carried Moses' decisions back to them. Many slanderers and envious people prevent the master's goodness from overflowing toward those worthy of it. So make this road easier, and drive away the slanderers, like bad dogs that bite in secret, with firm words as stones, so that all may gratefully keep an auspicious memory of you.

The future time will praise you and, as is fitting, receive your benefactions without forgetfulness. As for me, this improvised praise, made in brief, for I have no leisure, being pulled from every side by concerns for the young men who study with me, promises to fashion for you a more complete thanksgiving when you yourself complete for us the desired grain allowance, so that we may not be more curtailed than the others, especially while we labor the most complete labors, labors that lead toward completion.

## Consequential Changes From First Pass

- Recovered and translated G003 from Gautier I instead of leaving the slot empty.

## Source And Revision Notes

- Gautier notes that this item predates Theophylact's appointment to Ohrid; he is still a rhetor in Constantinople.
- The patriarch is not securely identifiable from the letter alone.
- Major allusions include Psalms 106/107:39, 147:3, 84/85:13, 87/88:7, 88/89:8, Job 5:18, Psalm 98/99:6, Exodus 13:21, Deuteronomy 4:24, Exodus 39:12, Psalm 120/121:6, 2 Corinthians 11:14, Jeremiah 23:23, Psalm 99/100:5, Psalm 114/116:5, Luke 6:36, Matthew 5:45, and Isaiah 54:2.
