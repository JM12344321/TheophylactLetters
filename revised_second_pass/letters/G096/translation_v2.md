## Revised English Translation

Come, hear, and I shall tell you, all who fear God, what the truly panhypersebastos has done for my soul - and now let even those who do not fear hear it. May the former advance in shared joy and look only to him in whom we too trusted and were not deceived; and may this all-good man serve what tends toward doing good with still greater effect.

When I had already been ranked among those who go down to Hades and was held fast by the darkness of despondency, he brought me back up to the light of life's cheerfulness and restored me. He made the emperor-sun warmer and more life-giving for me. He did not allow wickedness to lift its head against truth and crown itself, although it had opened its mouth, chirping and boasting that it would carry off our affairs like abandoned eggs.

For such were the thoughts of wickedness through its servants. Saying, "Let us magnify our tongue; our lips are our own; who is lord over us?" they spat the poison of asps against us, embittered the emperor's most transparent soul, and made the affairs of our Church dead. The cause of all these things was the greed and malice of the tax officials. Unable to bear the blade of my tongue cutting the sinews of their injustice, they conceived toil and gave birth to lawlessness.

First they approached our mighty emperor in person against my nothingness, giving substance to things without substance, adorning phantoms more pompously, and testifying that I possessed invincible power. They persuaded him that the hundred-handed men of myth - Briareus or Typhon with his hundred heads - were nonsense compared with me. They did this so that they might hinder my frank speech, whose sharpness entered their hearts, made them shudder, and drew back their hands.

Then, through intermediaries, those men worthy of Tartarus raised slander up to heaven. They strengthened against us Lazaros, who, as a paroikos of the Church and thinking too freely, longed to shake the yoke of paroikia from his neck. They taught him what and how to say, filled the emperor's ears, and blackened the heart that had until then been white toward us.

Once this Lazaros saw that his fabrications were running with the current, while the facts of truth were being forced upstream for us, he did not stop using his nature against us - and that nature is Bulgarian, a nurse of every evil. It was not enough for him to bring men of Ohrid as allies in the war against us. He also wandered through the other regions of Bulgaria, searching very carefully whether there was anyone condemned against us for heresy, or bound by ecclesiastical bonds as a bigamist or unlawful husband. When he found many such men, he brought them against us as though they were men of Ohrid who had been robbed by us of fields and vineyards, though their souls had been worn out by their own faults. Others whom he found in the city, needing the emperor for other reasons, he persuaded to join the outcry, so as to defame my name. He knows he strikes me on the temples if only he can make public cries against the archbishop while invoking the emperor; through them my reputation is befouled - or, to speak with the Spirit, made to dwell in the dust.

Recently he claimed that he had been burned out by me, although he had not been burned out at all by anyone and had nothing left to lose. Since he does no work, but meddles; since he has made it his craft to attach himself to the tax officials and, like dogs at tables and messmates, to look only for whatever may be thrown him by them, barking first and over-barking later, he is barer than a crab. Even if he had been burned out, it would have nothing to do with me, no more than events in Libya have to do with the Indians at Nysa. I was away from Ohrid when he says he suffered that dreadful non-event; in another way I was staying in Pelagonia, held down by serious illness.

What obscure thing would he not say and be believed with pledges, when he marches openly against things so plain? Though the emperor is a very well-sealed man and not easily given to false tales, the utterly wicked fellow softened him all the same. What posture did he not assume, what did he not lament, what oath did he spare, until he had magnified the burning and persuaded him that he had suffered these things from the archbishop as retribution for the goodwill which that man had sheared away?

He even swore that roads pour with cheese for me and that my mountains pour with milk; that I am rich and deep in wealth through the accumulation of I do not know how many talents; that I live like a satrap - indeed, that Median splendor, compared with archiepiscopal splendor, would appear paltry, and the royal halls at Susa and Ecbatana mere huts beside my many-storied and ventilated houses, in which I spend the summer cooling the furnace of the flesh that appears upon me. All this is a fabrication of a demonic judgment and a phantom of a tongue that has learned only to speak evil. Apart from the much-discussed land, I, mighty in strength and rival of the Aloadae, have not been found holding any public property, although I have been searched and examined maliciously.

Let no one condemn me in absence by immediately accepting the tax official's slanders against me as trustworthy. Whatever he says, let him also wait for the defenses from the side of the Church. This is what I especially ask of your Majesty. I ask nothing new, nothing unusual to the holy and most divine soul of the emperor, but only that what he preserves in every case and at all times be preserved in my case too: that he not be preempted by the words of an enemy and avenger.

If the man says that he can bring gains to the treasury from the clerics, "Hippocleides does not care." I am not accountable for this. First, they were not previously profited from us; each man enjoyed exemption for one yoke-team, and beyond that they paid the public treasury. Second, before this they had exemption from the tithe on all animals. No grateful judge would call this theft from the public treasury, but an undefined imperial generosity enjoyed abundantly by those who found it. Now, with that generosity receiving a limit and a known fixation, what lies beyond the definition has reasonably been added to the public treasury and has increased the weight of the demand.

Third - and this is worthy of tears - most of them did not enjoy even the exemption for one yoke-team or the defined exemption on animals. Because the man is more a condemnatory register-maker than a registrar, to say something ridiculous, he added yoke-teams and animals to them, and they were not only demanded payment on things for which they were entitled to remain exempt, but over-demanded. They suffered the same lawless treatment in other headings too. On mills the clerics paid double what laymen paid; and on the things called strougai in the Bulgarian language, which a Greek would call channels, used for catching fish, they too were fined many times more than the common multitude.

The "faithful and pious" man contrived these things so that the gains brought to the public treasury from the clerics might be swollen, and so that I might be slandered as ungrateful and greedy, preserving so great a loss for my benefactor and helper. I say the same about the taxes he demanded from the people of the Church. These too were demanded most unjustly: monks, consecrated eunuchs, and even a few laymen, not more than five, I think - all of them receiving nothing from the public treasury. How could they be judged fit to be taxed? Not even an emperor can do this.

He collected from me personally in such abundance that he did not dishonor the magnitude of my rank. For mills long since demolished he demanded everything; for those in existence he demanded twice as much as from the Bulgarians. For a small marshy spot that gave a poor catch of fish, he forced me to pay thirteen units, though I took from there fish worth no more than a single diobol, while everyone else paid one diobol for similar places. Although the Church possessed exemption, by imperial gift, for large and small animals, the men just mentioned were tithed on the few animals they had, which I had given them as a small consolation.

To pass over most things, lest I be counted the proverbial Arab flute-player, he made useless in every respect the order recently issued by your goodness. Regarding the estate, he frightened the villagers in countless ways, saying he would impose taxes on each of them beyond their capacity if they should choose the archbishopric again as master. He committed another wrong by not handing over to the Church the surplus persons in the estate, for whom he is obliged to carry out such an imperial order. When we asked that these people be registered according to each person's means and then handed over to us, he grew savage and, taking offense at our request alone, said: "Even if I ask that the first be paid by the Church as the seventh at the time of completion, you will pay so much unwillingly. Who has resisted my will? Who will judge me if I value what is worth so much at this much?" Yet I know the wisest Judith applied to God alone the saying, "You spoke, and they came to be; you thought, and they stood present." But this man makes himself a partner in that blessedness - who is he, what is he, how great is he, how mighty, beneath whom they will spread decay?

He adds this too: even in the case of the paroikoi given to the Church, whom he very unjustly registered and increased, he will grant the Church nothing except the single yoke-team, and he threatens to retain all the other headings himself. The order concerning the handing over of the estate plainly does not intend this. He does these things so that, disgusted by them, I may become more inactive about the consolation of the estate, and from there his most charming companion Lazaros may become master of these people.

In handing over the land, he shortens the measure of the rope and also behaves with utter ignorance in the calculation of modioi. How could he know the principles of geometry? Why do I register the number of sand grains and drops of rain? In every way, place, cause, time, matter, and person, this most just man engineers losses and harms for me, and contrives to rub stains upon me through which he knows his own plans are most likely to succeed.

For all these things I ask, as I said, that a place for defense be kept for me too, and that his words not be received as oracles. When the shadows are dissolved, truth will tear away the lion-mask and show the ugly monkey sitting underneath. Above all, I ask that an order be issued allowing me unhindered departure or rescue from here, so that I may not be swallowed up by despondency; for my strength is not the strength of stones. May my soul be taken untimely if, in this matter at least, I have in any way acted contrary either to God or to your Majesty.
