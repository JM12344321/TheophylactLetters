## Revised English Translation

You behave toward us as the Mycenaeans did toward Orestes, and we are so hated by you that you do not even address us. I, however, am so far from thinking anything unusual toward you that I both address you and make my customary jokes. They are brief, but of such a kind that they ought to be judged more like oracles than common words.

A furnace is being kindled against you: the jokes about your slowness. You do not merely supply vine-shoots and tow, nor even the tear of the pine; naphtha is transported to you from beyond the borders, and with it you water the furnace. So much do you desire to see the flame rise to something more magnificent.

If you release Tornikios, whom I have as a son-in-law through my niece, from his stay in the camp, you will have poured the whole Nile upon the flame that has now risen. If you do not, you bring in even the spring of that wondrous naphtha from Babylon. Have I spoken in riddles? Or do you have no need of Oedipus? I think you do not. In every other respect, who is sharper than you? And you would understand your own affairs more than others would.

But may you be kept above the other evils, and above that dreadful beast, slowness - whether you have been caught by it willingly, which I do not believe, or unwillingly. May you always be reported to me as being raised from glory to glory.
