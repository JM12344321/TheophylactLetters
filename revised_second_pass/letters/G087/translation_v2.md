## Revised English Translation

We received your letter, most honored brother, in which you present yourself to us as grateful and confess us to be benefactors, better than father and mother and the rest of your relatives. You also asked to receive release from the bond imposed on you by the brotherhood that assembled with us, because you did not assemble with it, although you had been summoned, and because you thereby hindered those managing necessary affairs.

At your show of gratitude we were weighed down beyond measure, because truth has so vanished from among us and deceit, with hypocrisy's cloak dyed in many colors, speaks openly on our stage and theatrically performs things worthy of horror: speaking peace with its neighbors while devising evils in the heart. For who does not know how, when your Honor entered the great city, you discharged countless missiles of slander against us, though in your letter you now register us as benefactors? Almost no one was left, greater or lesser, whose ears were not deafened by the disparagements against us that came from your tongue. There are not a few witnesses to these things, and they are not untrustworthy.

But to dramatize and lament these matters would exceed both speech and occasion, especially when we reason from them that the times of apostasy are not far off and that the threatened things belonging to them will not delay. Therefore we lift our eyes to God alone, who is able to stop these things, and to the mountains of his righteousness, from where help will come to us.

He who taught us by both what he did and what he said to be grateful - why do I say grateful? even to supply good things to those who do us harm - will either correct what is crooked, or he will surely weep when he sees it uncorrected, as his law from above declares. These things answer the way in which you presented yourself to us in a newly humble and unaccustomed tone.

As for your remaining request, we have admitted it as disciples of Christ, though unworthy ones. When he was reviled, he did not revile in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but handed matters over to the one who judges justly. He found fault with Peter's smallness when Peter thought it enough to forgive the sinner seven times, and he himself, rich in mercy, generously supplied the seventy-times-seven.

Accordingly, as I have said, we have accepted your request - especially since, as you wrote, you are bound by a serious illness and the danger of death stretches over you. So far as it lies with us, know that you are loosed in the Holy Spirit and may perform every episcopal work freely.

Yet since the bond was imposed on you by several brothers as well, as the letter issued to you synodically indicated, I have written these things both to the bishop of Stroumitza and to the bishop of Malesobe, through whose territories your servant will presumably pass. I know that your request will not be rejected by them either, and that they will release the bond they imposed, moved by one and the same Spirit. We shall write to the others too, and you will certainly learn from them what will seem good to them.

Our part, then, as we said, is released. For the rest, pray for us: bless those who love you, and do not curse. Be ashamed to fall so far short of the Gospel command, which wishes us to bless even those who persecute us and not to curse.
