## Revised English Translation

Will you too suffer what most people suffer, God-honored brother and master, and blame the present letter, which comes to you in a time of necessity and has not come before now because, clearly, no necessity had arisen calling for help? Or, since you are nurtured together with reason and give all things to judgment, will you not find fault with those who, in time of illness, call the one able to heal, while judging it superfluous to trouble him whenever the body is in good health?

This, I think - or rather, I am securely persuaded of it, unless I have forgotten your steadiness. Since this is how matters stand with us, listen now to the subject of the little letter.

An estate belonging to the Church under us, lying around Bardarion, has become a traveling provision for the most godless tax officials against our injury, especially for Iasites, whose greed is incurable and who knows neither how to honor human beings nor God. A harsh tongue has been given to him. Using wicked neighbors of ours - one might call them nets and hunters of everything - as accomplices in what his soul wanted, he seized almost everything belonging to the estate. He devoured me and divided me up, acting most irrationally as if angry at reason itself.

Since, then, as we have learned, the authority around Mardarion has now been entrusted to the son of our all-venerable lord, I have written to our common lord so that he may take us up in his usual way and suggest better things concerning us to his beloved son. He will act kindly if, in other respects too, he keeps us unharmed regarding this estate. And he will grant this favor not only to us but to God: that he not send registrars upon the estate. Even the most inhuman of tax officials granted us this.

These things, then, we asked of our lord in the letter to him. We also write to the Honor of your Holiness so that you may follow the same course, render the service due to love and to the God of love, and perform this ministry: show our letter to the all-venerable man, and continually spur him on to bring the requests made through the letter to completion.

I seem troublesome to you, admirable brother. But what can I do, when I have no power to fight necessity, which the proverb has made stronger even than the divine race? Take up this labor, then, and become for us a Heracles who kills robbers; for I call the registrars robbers of truth. May God, to whom you have consecrated yourself both by reason and by profession, keep you above every corruption and injury.
