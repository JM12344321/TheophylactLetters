## Revised English Translation

You are telling me my own dream, most longed-for of men, when you say that you yourself are being barbarized in the midst of Bulgarians. Consider how much I have drunk from the mixing-bowl of rusticity after so many years away from the places of wisdom, and how drunk I have become with boorishness.

Who will give us the opposite intoxication, to become drunk from the mixing-bowl of wisdom, the one Solomon mixes, making those who were formerly foolish into the wise? How often I have said to her, "Show me your face, and let me hear your voice!" But she flees from me and hides herself, and all the more pricks my longing. As for me, I am an incurable lover, in danger of becoming a fable in life: like the lover of Echo, whether he was a spirit or a god, or like the much-talked-of man who always thirsted and was always deceived about the drink.

Again Solomon's saying comes upon me: "I said, I will become wise; and she went far from me, farther than she was." In saying these things I am not doing anything other than composing tragedy over sufferings like my own. Some cure, indeed, must be contrived at great cost.

For us, since we have long remained in Bulgaria, rusticity has already become of one manner and one hearth with us. But you should not pamper yourself by speaking ironically toward private men. You let the books fall from your hands only yesterday, so that the echoes of the wise are still present in you. In any case I have now sent you a book of Chrysostom. May Wisdom itself, Christ, the fountain of wisdom, give you understanding and lead you together on the road by which you travel, teaching his knowledge along with practice.
