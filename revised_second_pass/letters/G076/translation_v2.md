## Revised English Translation

Know that this brief time during which you have been silent is measured by me in centuries. So much do I love your speech, you who outshine everyone for me. When your letter appears, it is to me like that phoenix-sun. But, blessed man, do not imitate that phoenix, a rare sight for the Egyptians. Imitate the sun instead, and shine on me every day with letters. Better still, make the day longer for me by otherwise concealing the night.

You will certainly not plead your work through the nights. If you do, you will do an injustice to your own truth, since you are able to supply both those labors and your letters to us. For in matters of discourse dexterity belongs no less to you than to Homer's Paean in matters of war. You breathe even improvised pieces more beautifully and more pleasantly than others produce their lamp-smelling compositions, curled, combed, and hung from sapphire in the ears. For the moment, perhaps, they seem something before a drunken theater; but when the sun has sprung up and left the lovely lake, we do not even see the dew.

If you needed time and cycles for the begetting of words, there would be some reason for your rare and long-delayed letters. But since, when you simply throw open the doors of your sober mouth, you utter golden oracles, you see how you wrong my longing, you who take forethought for Justice herself.

Do not do so from now on. Send me your Zephyrs for a soul scorched badly by heat. You know our sicknesses, and especially the present ones, when the Lord has laid a burning heat upon us and melted away all life-giving moisture. That one heat, the only thing I had left to fight, I warded off with iambic verses, after it had forced me to sing many elegies through the whole year. But since provocations and slanders will never leave us - I do not know whether because of the necessary revolution of the present atmosphere or because our sins have gone up to God - do not cease providing us with refreshment of soul. Certainly the Zephyrs will not leave you either before fragrance leaves the spice-bearing plant.
