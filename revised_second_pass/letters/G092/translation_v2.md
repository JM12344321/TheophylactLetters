## Revised English Translation

Caught between two emotions, my most magnificently distinguished son and lord in the Lord, I do not know to which of them I should incline myself more. When I heard that you were returning from Colchis, I was divided between pleasure and pain. Pleasure, because we now have you nearer; the imperial city will enjoy you, coming into spring through your presence after the winter of your absence; and the emperor, the strongest of all in all things, will have a sleepless guard, a partner in common deliberations and cares, and a most genuine counselor.

Pain, because now the barbarian will not even doze at the ring of his shield - no, nor even on his horse - but will recline comfortably on a couch, stretch out his feet, and breathe easy dreams. He will not flee Taronites even in apparitions. The cities of the Pontus will now truly have a sea of evils. After the calm that came in your time and from you, the storm that preceded you will fall on them again and swell their trouble beyond soothing. This is more painful for the wretched; for misfortune, when interrupted by prosperity and then returning to itself, becomes more grievous, just as cold after warmth is colder.

Unhappy beyond unhappy are those for whom the abundance of good things they enjoyed is transformed into an excess of misery. They sprang up again in the temperate season of your hour, and then all at once were dried out. They have become what they were at the beginning, as though you had not ruled them - except that now they have this additional burden: to feel their misfortunes more deeply and to have been deprived of better hopes.

Whom will they hope to find from now on who will preside over civic affairs as if he did not even know the name of war, yet excel in military affairs so heroically that, without merely looking at the city, he will impoverish the enemy and enrich the subject? Who will make soldiers winged as they go out to war, but heavy and slow-moving as they return, light in eagerness and weighed down with spoils? Under whose abundance will a part of the Church find rest and have a child to support its old age, believing that he is strengthened by blessings and eager in turn to support with bodily things? Whose chastity will astonish even the monks of the desert, as they see themselves surpassed in bodily purity by as much as they see fire, in so great a body, denying nature?

But grief has almost made me a tragedian. Enough has been paid to those people by brotherly love. Since you have drawn near to those who long for you, and all enjoy you so far as they can, and since I myself, if anyone, am worthy to enjoy you, do not measure out to me any longer, at intervals, the graces that come from Scripture. Do not endure letting joy drip to us drop by drop. Flood my lowliness with whole rivers, and make your affairs known to me more frequently, since I am so hopelessly in love with you. From now on you will surely not blame the Pontus or an impassable distance.

Send better and sweeter news about yourself, so that in this way at least you may cool my tongue, like that of the rich man, as I suffer pain from the injuries of the slanderers in this other unquenchable flame. Whenever I hear anything of yours, I receive it like rain upon scorched earth. I hear such things each time from Theodosios, who tells them with a grateful tongue, though one unequal to the praise that your deeds deserve. He is with us and grants us the greatest gifts by hymning your excellence. May the Lord grace it still more and more, and always show you brighter in every virtue of soul and body, and keep you above every corruption.
