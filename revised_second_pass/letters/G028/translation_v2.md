## Revised English Translation

Has the wise proedros had even his tongue bound? Alas for the present times, which give gout power to bind tongues too; in this way they drive everything toward the worse.

But perhaps this has not been contrived by the times, nor have you yourself suffered it. Perhaps you are dedicating to our brother the conversations that would have come to us, leading him and giving him understanding on the road by which he travels toward philosophy. I accept this; indeed, I accept it more gladly than every conversation directed to me.

See, then, that you converse more continually with the young man, if you care to gladden me more often. Let him enjoy the common mixing-bowls of your wisdom, and let him partake with the rest of the noble lovers of learning. Yet I want something private also to come to him from that wisdom, so that he may have some profit which is secretly bestowed on him. Mothers too, when they take one of their own children aside, often set before him some sweetmeat privately.

Do you wish me to give you a rule for what I mean? As much as Theophylact has been singled out by you for friendship above the rest, so many private graces do you owe to my brother. And so that I may turn it back upon you: however many rational graces you deposit with my half, my dear charge, by so much will I show Theophylact loved by you above the others.

I will show another thing. Whatever you would wish to be for Paul, if some mystery of wisdom were being performed for him by me, be that for my Demetrios as he is initiated by you into the rites of philosophy. Live robustly and cheerfully, most gracious of men to me.
