## Revised English Translation

How could I tell you, most honored master, how gladly I received your letter? Everything is good in its proper time, but a word most of all - and especially a word born from such a mind and delivered by such a tongue. When I was sunk in despondency, you poured over me that drug which makes grief forgotten; when I was thirsty, you sweetened me with the stream from the rock. Like an interpreter of the Paraclete, you comforted my soul in its pain.

For we were weighed down beyond measure, so that we despaired even of life: battles outside, fears within. The enemies have grown strong; as for friends and neighbors, some have opposed us, and others, in the more humane course, have stood at a distance. Yet he who comforts the lowly and heals the broken in heart has comforted us through those with whom he has spoken. The dew that came from you became healing for us. Thanks be to the Father of mercies and God of all consolation, because in such evil days, in times friendless and beast-like, he has given us your Holiness, affectionate toward us in brotherly love and justified in the bowels of Jesus.

Do not grow weary, then, man of God, in using toward us the judgment and wisdom with which the Lord has enriched you, and in supplying abundantly to those in need the treasure which you possess in an earthen vessel, the body of humility. As for me, neither the revenues in which the strength of the archbishopric once lay grieve me, nor the authority and jurisdiction stretched from rivers to the ends of the sea. We have enough of the former; and we have learned from what source surplus comes. The latter is too weak a thing to hold fast. What wastes my bones and consumes my marrow is the flood of cares, the wicked distraction, the useless disturbance, and the failure yet to find rest from evil days.

For before we have fully escaped the lion, a bear meets us; and when we flee that too and brace our hands against the wall of the house, a serpent looks out treacherously from the hole. This is what makes my pains sharper. Yet although I greatly wish to spit these things out and lighten my heart a little, I hide them in a spirit that has learned to endure. Even now I set a guard on my mouth, even when the sinner stands before me, lest I shame the great reality - or even the name - of the high priesthood. A letter, as you know, is a talkative thing and no keeper of secrets.

For the nets of our afflictions have been stretched out by fellow priests and fellow bishops, by those who are thought to judge the people. But you, honored father and master, pray that we may escape the dangers that arise among these false brothers, and that they themselves may sober up from the snare of the devil in which they have been captured alive. Above all, pray that the one set against us may be put to shame, and that through all things God may be glorified. May his grace be with your spirit, all-holy and sacred master.
