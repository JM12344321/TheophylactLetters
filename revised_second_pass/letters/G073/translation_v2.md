## Revised English Translation

Holy master, your letters always drip upon me the dew of consolation; but the one recently brought to me flooded my heart with whole rivers of despondency, teaching me again how badly your body is faring and how your soul has been swallowed by the sea-monster of grief because of the death of the sebastokrator, incomparable in all things.

At once I looked up to God, who is able to heal both. Since he is Father of the soul and Creator of the body, he will still the former as a child that is whimpering, with the brightest soothing words, and he will strengthen the creature by giving it support for its strength. As soon as I looked to him, the greater inrush of despondency was cut short. And when I turned my gaze also toward your greatness of soul and considered the strength that heals pain, the ark came to rest for me upon dry land.

I reasoned that the greatness of your mind will make what has happened into material for better things, and will regard the suffering of the body as a reminder - as indeed it is - to learn not to give everything to that body, whose outflow we cannot contrive to stop even if we rise to the highest pitch of wisdom and power, the one devising and the other providing what might stop it. Rather one must give more earnestly to that which is more divine and more masterly in us, so as not to say the whole ministry of our better part: the part appointed first to rule the world within us and then the irrational things outside us; the part that alone we possess as firm, and, because it comes from what does not flow away and cannot perish, itself unflowing and imperishable.

As for the death of your kinsman, whose gifts of goodness I myself have experienced more than anyone, he is to be mourned by me too, if by anyone. Yet accept this as a preparation. I believe that you always, according to the saying of the writer of Proverbs, you who possess wisdom no less than rule, prepare your works for the departure. You make the very nature of the body, and simply every unstable judgment - possessing only the ever-moving as its immovable thing - like forerunners of what will certainly come. Especially now, as if they were nearer forerunners and, to speak more intimately, torchbearers, you are persuaded that the expected one is all but present, and for that reason you prepare yourself more, so as to secure for yourself freedom from disturbance.

Thinking these things about your royal self - resourceful as it is, arranging all things and turning even bitter things toward sweetness and unseemly things toward what is fairer - I see that not even mud has been left in my heart from the flood of the many waters of despondency. The sun dried everything when I merely thought how you would be disposed toward what had happened.

Indeed, why should you not receive every unpleasant thing pleasantly because of your greatness of soul, since you even judge my words, which are more unpleasant than almost anything, sweeter than all things? You, the eagle, confess yourself charmed by the croaking of the crow; and you believe that the fish from our parts, made pleasant in the same way by my words, can rouse an appetite deadened to everything else.

You speak in a way worthy of your goodness, lifting yourself higher by the very things through which, in condescension, you raise up my lowliness. We know ourselves to have nothing charming in either words or deeds. Nevertheless fish have been sent: some, preserved as salted loaves; others, baked with little breads. May my conversation, which I make through this letter, be sweetened for you. Still more may the fish be sweetened to your appetite, as both a sending and a blessing from the one who bore for us in the flesh the giver of life. May they revive it and become strengthening for the body, leading it back to steadiness in its natural operations. Their number too is a symbol of natural perfection, if ten is the perfect number and nature is fivefold through matter contemplated in the four elements and the form from which all natural things in our condition arise.
