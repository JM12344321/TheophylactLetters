## Revised English Translation

The Word of God has benefited us in many other ways, most honored brother and fellow-minister, and has granted us this too: to be present even where we are not present. Although we have a nature bounded by place, at least in what is visible, by the power of this grace we become almost unbounded. A man in Egypt can be in Scythia, neither leaving his fatherland nor intruding into the foreign land in a novel way. Letters produce this marvel.

So I too, though absent, am now present through the body of letters, most holy brother. I speak, I greet you, and I exhort you toward the work that belongs to us. That work is speech, the meditation on divine commandments and histories, from which the fire of longing for virtue is kindled, if David is a worthy witness when he says, "In my meditation a fire will be kindled."

This fire consumes every wooden and brush-like material. Whatever in you is golden and divine - and we believe the mind certainly to be such a thing - it will make bright and cleanse and show free from every leaden admixture. "Was not our heart burning within us," Scripture says, "as he spoke to us on the way and opened the Scriptures to us?"

The Lord now walks with you also on the road of life and is with you through the dignity of high priesthood. If you are willing to listen, he speaks in the prophets, in the apostles, in the evangelists, and in the ancient histories. When you receive the way he speaks to you, your heart will burn, and coals will be kindled from him: the remnants and memory of the things spoken and read to you.

But perhaps you will say that there is dark water in the clouds of the air, that is, obscurity and darkness in the scriptural clouds. Look, however, at what follows: from the radiance before him the clouds pass by. When he shines in the ruling part of us, those dark clouds do not remain; they pass by, indeed dissolve.

He spoke parables to the disciples and the crowds, like clouds containing dark water. The disciples, by asking, received his radiance, and the clouds dissolved for them under the sunbeams of the Lord's voice. The crowds, unwilling to labor and ask for the interpretations, found the parables to be clouds indeed. Therefore, seeing they did not see, and hearing they did not understand.

Since the commandment is radiant, since it enlightens the eyes, since the law is a lamp and a light, do not hesitate to open your eyes. You will certainly see the light, not the whole of it perhaps, but as much as is possible; for we do not see the whole sun either, and yet, as much as we do see, it is sweeter to us than everything.

Think, too, that David is speaking to you: "I have prepared a lamp for my Christ." He has set before you his law ready at hand, both in letter and in spirit, since you are a Christ by the chrism of the priesthood, fragrant and precious. Do not despise this ready lamp, so that, shining upon your feet, it may direct your steps and make you a perfect child of perfect light, keeping you above every darkness of evil.
