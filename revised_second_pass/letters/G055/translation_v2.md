## Revised English Translation

For a long time, most revered son in the Lord, I have been lamenting my lot and calling it malicious and ill-starred, because it carried me away and cast me into this farthest borderland, where envy and rancor dwell, and the other deadly spirits too; where the voice of reason is hated more than perfume by beetles. But now I am being led to recognize even graces in it: it gave me residence among these barbarians, and through that residence I have gained you as a hearer. This is something that in the great city, though valued more than everything by many, would scarcely have been granted to a few.

The lot has fallen for me more fortunately than it did for Plato. He sailed to Dionysius in the hope that, by fitting a naturally gifted young man, easy for his teacher to handle, with the bridles of reason, he might join philosophy to kingship and give to the power of doing the greatest things also the will to choose the best. Yet he came so far short of his hopes that Dionysius, who had never before sinned against philosophy, after Plato's conversation about justice and beauty committed the most shameful outrage against it. Those who are not ignorant of Pollis and Anniceris know the outrage.

I, however, have not cut through a long sea nor measured out the whole deadly Charybdis. Living in this little house, I enjoy your greatness of mind; and because you lend me your hearing, a Lydian chariot carries me along. In this I am far more fortunate than Plato.

Therefore we shall not cower even before the public Briareus. Against those hundred hands of his we shall set the right hand of justice, and through that hand we shall make power. I am not, my most revered care of the Word, compelling you as a matter of necessity to relax what is just. I make this voluntary for you; I entrust it to you rather than impose it on you. But there is another thing for which I beg you unavoidably, more urgently than the very taxpayers beneath you pay their taxes: do not relax at all in regard to justice, nor innovate against it through love of the greater gain.

Let me distinguish for you what many fail to know. We human beings live either according to justice, or contrary to justice, or above justice. By the first we are preserved as human; by the second we are refashioned into beasts; by the third we are made like gods. You would be altogether beautiful, then, if you painted your own soul with the divine colors of the life above justice. In the office over which you have been set, this would mean, from the outset, being able - or choosing - to disregard even what has been remitted by the emperor. But if this is not wholly possible or not desired, we shall not force you, wingless as you are, to be borne in the air.

We shall ask for what is human; and if you are not eager for it of yourself, we shall prick you with rational goads. When you are thus moved, we shall move you with the middle motion, and I will boldly call it royal, for this is surely what the emperor also wishes. Then Briareus will not stretch out his hands, though he has reared them boldly; he will step forward in due order, and, thinking in a manner worthy of your nobility, you will spit out the baseness of a life that is brutal and unworthy of humanity.

Do you see how moderate I am in my request, so that in this too I may teach you also to be moderate in the commands you give to those subject to you? May the Lord, who directs us toward the path of justice and lifts those who wish it into the airy course above justice, who entrusts to us the greatest offices and gives still greater things in return for moderation within them, himself instruct you more wisely, give you understanding in every way, and keep you above every evil treatment and every harm.
