## Revised English Translation

If you had heard, my all-holy lord, that the archbishop had lost his senses and was sick with an incurable madness, then you ought to have believed that he both disobeyed your letter and did not even deign to write back. But if in other matters we are indeed senseless, having become like beasts through the passions, corrupted by them, and made loathsome by practices unlike God, in this matter at least we retain our senses: we know the eminence on which you stand and by which you surpass all.

Do not so readily believe those who speak against us. Let the lips of those who slander us before your goodness be made speechless. Let them show to whom they gave the note concerning the bishopric and who delivered it to us. Then they may establish what they say, that we failed to answer out of contempt. By the prayer of your holy mother, investigate to whom the note was given. If I am found to have received it and not replied, I shall be held accountable as contemptuous.

Since I have defended myself concerning the charge that I did not receive my lord's letter about a bishop, behold, I now write about the bishopric itself. It is not fitting for you, my lord, to thrust yourself into such matters; they are great and fearful. Nor is it fitting for us simply to give away divine things as favors.

Among those whom we have advanced to bishoprics, some served in this Church and were attested for reverence and seriousness, such as the bishops of Morobos and Pristhiane. Others shone in Constantinople by word and teaching, such as the bishops of Kastoria and Belgrade. Others distinguished themselves in a monastery by the monastic life, such as the bishop of Triaditza. In these ways they obtained the archpriestly dignity.

If the man recommended by my lord is such a one, it is not you who will owe us thanks, but we who will owe thanks to you. But if he is neither known to our Church, nor distinguished among those in Constantinople who are attested for word or life, do not wish vainly and idly to offend God, and do not command us to do this, since we have been ordered to obey God rather than human beings.

Furthermore, we say this too: no bishopric is now lacking for us, unless someone names Vidin. Even though we long greatly to find a man worthy of it, we cannot. It requires a man well compacted, quick in both spiritual and worldly matters.

How, then, did I disobey my lord, and how did I not write to Proximos about sending the cleric? Did I not send that man earlier, even though, deceived by the Egyptian, he returned? And again, a second time, I sent him together with the men of the tax-service. Who is it that turns your pure judgment against us? Some deceitful man, it seems; indeed, perhaps the devil. May the Lord shatter whoever he is, breaking his wickedness and healing the man himself. And may he preserve you for us as a river, or rather as pure water.
