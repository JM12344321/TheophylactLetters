## Revised English Translation

"How did you dare," someone might say, "to speak frankly to the master, you who for so many years shut your own lips with the guard of silence and said nothing, small or great, to your benefactor? You ought to have embraced him with mouth, spirit, and letter, and through every possible means to have held him holy. For he is not only master, but father, indeed the most familiar and affectionate of fathers."

If I am the soul, the patriarch acquired and shaped this soul for me, shaping it daily and savingly; and besides this, he has both the nature and the training to be compassionate toward our weaknesses. Perhaps he himself too, having been tested and having learned from what he suffered, knows our condition. I need say no more: if these things happened in the green wood, what will they not do in the dry? Or perhaps by the greatness of his mind he embraces even what he did not know by personal suffering.

For he is not ignorant of the thorns - ah, how shall I say it? - that now pierce those who exercise high priesthood, nor of the brambles in which we have been caught and turned toward misery. The boasting of sinners sets its head against heaven while they kill widow and orphan and murder every native-born person and stranger. There is no one to help or rescue. Everything is dragged and carried away, with no one ransoming or saving.

They will madly lord it over us; and our collectors set their traps sharper than a sickle against what remains. All those placed over us are young at once in age and mind, but always younger still in injustice: they neither grow old nor decline, but each time invent some new form of greed, renewing crookedness in their inward parts, restrained neither by admonitions nor by fear of human beings, but looking toward something stronger by which they find wickedness and are reared in injustice [the public PG/OCR packet breaks off here].
