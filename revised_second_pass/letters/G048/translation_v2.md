## Revised English Translation

You ask how our affairs are. Not very well; that is the milder way to say it. Fever smolders within, already on the point of advancing into something malignant. Dry coughs trouble me, without phlegm. A pain in the side has lasted a long time. The summit of my evils is this: cares beyond telling and beyond consolation, about the people in common, about ecclesiastical affairs in common, about everything here.

They even shoot at me from beyond the borders. Though I sit in Achrida, I am struck by arrows from Glavenitza, from Vidin, or from Slanitza. You yourself must endure a little of the injustice of barbarian names, so that you may not feast entirely on the names of the Greeks. From one place and another, arrow after arrow has stuck into my heart, and there is no Paieon, no second Machaon or Podaleirios, to pull out the shaft, suck the blood, and sprinkle soothing medicines upon it. Instead it lies embedded, producing heavy or sharp pains.

Such is my condition - and know that you have heard only the ten-thousandth part of our affairs. This too I suffer badly among the rest: I am not even permitted to say how I am. May you be for me among things that are easier, so that, grieving in common among common griefs, in the phrase of wise Sophocles, I may not receive the evil doubled.

May the Lord, the only giver of life, who breathes into dry bones and gives them movement, grant to us some moderate relief from griefs, and to you repose among all things more cheerful.
