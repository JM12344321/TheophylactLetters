## English Translation

Most honored lord, after I returned from my laborious and many-day wandering - for even now I have measured out [a whole Charybdis] and worn down the camp, so eagerly does the one who from the beginning is by nature the cause of toil contrive that we should fare very badly - a letter from your holiness fell into my hands on my return. It appeared so sweet that not even a stern wind from astern seems sweeter to sailors when they are weary from well-planed oars; or, if you prefer, like a spring to a thirsty deer.

Do not ever deprive me of this beautiful speech of yours. Always, again and again, shoot your voice into my ears, which is the same as saying your gladness. For in places so without consolation, or rather workshops of every wickedness, if you do not feed us these sweet little loaves, which are like a divine hand and good teachers, what remains for us except to flow down into Hades and run off toward every evil?

Do not cease, then, striking in this way at grief and wickedness, the two hands of the evil one, by which he is always eager to seize us, contriving something against us. These things, then, you yourself, though we are unworthy, will continue to provide; and may God grant it as we have hoped.

As for the holy men of Serres, I do not myself know from what causes, or how they are being tossed by the spirit of anarchy. Yet I have no way to calm that spirit. No one among those in the monastery seems to me worthy of such oversight, and no outsider is readily available. I judge, therefore, that one must choose the better part of the evil, since the good is not present.

I write these things while looking from far away. If I am compelled to go up, then, standing nearer to the affairs themselves, I shall do for the monastery what God wills through your prayers. Strike, strike the accursed enemy of our salvation with the weapons of your holiness, snatching my weakness out of his hand.

## Translator's Notes

- The opening is damaged but the emotional structure is secure: exhaustion, consolation by letter, then a practical monastery problem.
- The "sweet little loaves" image is Theophylact's affectionate metaphor for the recipient's written words.
