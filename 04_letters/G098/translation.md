## English Translation

I have been helped by your incomparable Greatness, and my flesh has flourished again. Thanks be to the one who has graced your charity, so that mercy and truth go before it. We have learned that by these every power is bounded. My soul had melted away like a spider; my life, to use Job's phrase, was lighter than talk; and, according to the prophet, consolation was hidden from my eyes. The sons of disobedience - and therefore of destruction - were without natural affection, implacable, and covenant-breaking. It is good to call them by names taken from Paul and from the Spirit.

They loved every word of drowning, every deceitful tongue. They bent their bow, a bitter thing, to shoot the blameless in secret. They shot indeed, and added to the pain of my wounds. But you took me up and did not make my enemies rejoice over me. You clothed yourself with zeal as with full armor; you divided crushing against strength, as the divine Amos says, and brought misery upon the stronghold of slander.

That stronghold was founded by wicked craft. The man called "Nothing" carried off first place as architect, laying heavy stones hard to move, gathering things spoken and unspoken against us and throwing them into the imperial ears. Those who came after him built on it and raised it high: the very men who dug down the things of Ohrid to the ground. Since I stood as a rebuke to them - I, the man excessive in everything and meddlesome, keeping away from their roads as from uncleannesses - even the sight of me was burdensome to them. Therefore they took counsel to thrust away my honor.

They ran in thirst and slandered me to our mighty emperor, your beloved brother, as a man of great and mighty power, enlarged by so many profitable revenues and heavy upon the roads. They contrived all this to cast me out from frank speech with him. Standing on that frank speech as on a very advantageous platform, I used to wrestle with them and force them to fall from their plots, using active hands through speech, and doing so in many ways according to the discernment demanded by stewardship.

For this they used Lazaros as their servant. You know the man - and who does not know him from the wrongs he has committed around me? He is like that notorious villain who, wanting to become famous from obscurity, burned the temple of Artemis at Ephesus. Through him they accomplished everything they wished. In all other respects he resembles idle drones; only for this does he find opportunity: to work with the tax officials, live at their table, receive from them clothes more respectable and cleaner than his own craft, and accept every other kindness. He makes my blame his food and luxury.

Perceiving that everything is more bearable to me than a blasphemed name, he plants his tongue, a sharp knife, in the most vital part of my affairs. Spewing the mud of his own soul into the imperial ears - how shall I say it? - he fills that man, sweet in goodness, with disgust, and makes him seasick before his abundant mercy toward us. Recently he claimed that he had been burned out by me, though he had suffered no burning at all from anyone and had, simply, nothing to give as fuel to the fire or to lose, no more than drones have a honeycomb. Nor was I in Ohrid when he says he suffered the burning. Yet he formed the accusation and gave shape to the shapeless thing with streams of tears, blows of his head against the ground, outcries of lamentation, and the rest of his counterfeit Bulgarian performances. He lamented that he had suffered all these dreadful things because he was loyal to the public treasury and had taken up war against archiepiscopal tyranny; and so he captured the emperor's sympathy utterly.

For this reason he was handed over to the tax official as sitter among tax officials and as lord over the one who rules us. Our affairs are carried this way and that: one man suggests monstrous plans, the other nourishes and fattens what has been suggested; one man sows tares in the purity of the Church's rights, not thirtyfold nor sixtyfold - for neither are we so fortunate nor is his soil so petty - but a hundredfold and more.

This "faithful and pious" man raises everyone against us, promising as wages for rebellion and accusation a sweet and desired lightening of the taxes laid upon them. What could a tax official not accomplish, with power to humble this man and exalt that one, and with such influence over the imperial heart because of the leaven of the Pharisees and the hypocrisy with which he has kneaded himself entirely, having no unleavened bread of sincerity and truth?

For all this, as holy Isaiah says, his anger has not turned away, but his hand is still high. Even now, having sent Lazaros up again, he is considering apostasy with certain inhabitants of the estate. He has taught them what they must say, attempting to render useless to the Church the emperor's generous order concerning the estate, and to make our help empty and profitless for us. Rather, he is trying to persuade the emperor, by lies he speaks from his own resources, to push aside the orders about the exchange and to consider the chrysobull issued upon them a spider's web. He forms conspiracies, digs pits, and describes how to hide a snare, saying, "Who will see him?" In short, he has treated us as though we were heretics.

Is it not dreadful if he remains tireless and adamantine in wickedness, while your goodness grows weary in accomplishing what is noble, and the ruler of this world appears to have more than the Almighty? I for one do not think so. Therefore your soul, hating evil, was indignant at the man's passion against us, and reminded our mighty and holy emperor of his own depth of judgment. Because of it, he will not condemn our wretchedness there on account of prejudice in favor of the tax official. He will keep one of his ears unspoiled for us also, doing what one of the Greek kings is praised for doing; and we have the Gospel, the beginning and completion of every good, teaching us: "Does your law judge a man unless it first hears from him and knows what he is doing?"

Nor will he surrender what he has set in order through firm and unreasoned orders to those who tear it down. May God, who rescues the wronged from a hand stronger than theirs, either there or by guiding affairs, keep for you in the future the fuller rewards for our rescue.
