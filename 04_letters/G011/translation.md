## English Translation

Since God has sent your protection to the poor, the members of God, my most venerable lord and protector, do not cease doing the thing for which you came out. In the words of the divine Arsenios, I say to you, who are most divine in nature and in education: in all other matters too, help the poor against those who take them captive, for that is the more fitting way to speak; and drive away this evil fate of theirs as well.

One of the monks and priests, a man from among my troubles, has despised God and become shameless even before human reproach. He has taken upon himself that prophetic phrase, the "face of a prostitute." He has cast off the holy habit. A glutton instead of a faster, licentious instead of self-controlled, or rather completely false in self-control, he lives with a woman and parades his debauchery without shame.

This is worthy of many tears, but it is still more bearable than what must now be said. Listen, then, to the thing that especially provokes God and grieves us, you who alone are able to correct it. Since he has shaken off divine fear and said to God, "I do not wish to know your ways," he longs and hastens to be powerful in wickedness, and becomes for the men of Iasippos the agent of unjust and unlawful exactions.

They cherish him. Why would they not? They all but carry him on their heads. The poor, meanwhile, are carried headlong and destroyed by false accusations, with no one to ransom or save them.

I sent for the accursed man and brought him to me. He gave me security that until the Lord's Days of the meat-fast no party of his choosing would be found or bring an accusation against anyone. I released him; and the Christians, the men of Iasippos, are cherishing him and keeping him as their hunter, for that is what he calls himself, offering to them the prey they desire.

Rise up over these matters, my lord. Speak the word, and the demon that harasses the people of God will be driven away. Let no one deceive you with empty words, saying that the thief will repent. How many medicines of repentance I brought to him last year and this year, both by letter and in person when I met him! But I was speaking to a deaf wave, and the leopard returned again to its spots.

Have mercy, then, on the poor, and before them on that man's soul, which is corrupted by wickedness before he corrupts others by his injuries. Let this pest and public disease be driven away from these members. If I myself am able to seize him, he will die in the tower as a common and public evil. May God preserve my holy lord above every evil and injury.

## Translator's Notes

- The language is intentionally severe. Theophylact frames a corrupt cleric as both moral scandal and instrument of fiscal/political oppression.
- The phrase "face of a prostitute" echoes prophetic invective; it should not be flattened into mere "shamelessness."
