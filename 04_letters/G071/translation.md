## English Translation

I have undergone toward you, good John, exactly what the story says the hares underwent toward the frogs. For all the rest of the time I lamented my own cowardice and because of it kept asking to be buried in the depths of the earth. But now, since I have found you more cowardly, I lift my neck and stride far ahead of you, like the hero of Salamis.

Why, admirable man, have you taken fright at the sight of affairs, when we, who endure their active wickedness, are cheerful, although we are utterly unprepared and untrained for it? You know what our life was like: inactive and useless. What else was it but the life of books and an ancient polity, with nothing suited to this new and active life? Yet I endured and remained. But where are you fleeing, throwing your shield behind you like a coward in the crowd? Why have you lost heart before the rattling of the chariots? Their horses have no rider, no chariot-companion, no fighter. The carts merely rush on, clattering, driverless and disorderly. Stand under the charge, resist, and at once they will cease their course. Already, once you have shouted them down, you will mount the horses, and your horsemanship will be salvation.

Have I perhaps recited riddles and enigmas to you, so that you need a solution? Or have I led you down into the labyrinth, so that you need Ariadne's thread for the ascent and exit? I do not think so. You know from where you are that these are Bulgarian affairs: whoever has courage at the beginning will see that they are images of war. Then he will gain mastery over them, save them, and the region will be saved as far as possible - all the more, the more he is eager to have God as ally. From Solomon we have learned that doing just deeds is more pleasing to him than the blood of sacrifices.

Through him may you yourself also please God, dearest and kindest of men to me, conducting yourself worthily of the reason that bore and nourished you, gladdening my ears, and holding as your own glory the good repute of your beloved son. May you have the just God stretching his mighty hand over your command and keeping you above every corruption and every harm.

## Translator's Notes

- The "hares and frogs" proverb makes Theophylact mock his own fear before chiding the recipient's.
