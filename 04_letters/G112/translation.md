## English Translation

I am a violent man toward you, always claiming to receive something from you. Yesterday I asked for your kindness toward our people there, and indeed received it - for I have not learned to sow on rocks. Now again I trouble your deep-soiled field and cast down the word of a request.

The request is that we may make use of some Galenic books, not so much for medical practice - I am not at the right age for learning the works of the art - as for knowledge and technical handling of doctrines. So whether you have monographs or explanations of Hippocratic writings, know that we wish to harvest these from you.

See, then, that no winged and premature conceit, no rock and unyielding hardness of soul, no thorns of excuses - setting before us the cares of technical work as an objection to the books - make our labor over the seed vain. Bear fruit for me as the good earth, thirtyfold and sixtyfold; and if we should receive the treatise on the doctrines of Hippocrates and Plato, we shall count you as having borne a hundredfold. The man who will bring me my fruit will be introduced to your Love by the all-excellent Smyrnaios. May the Father of Love itself keep that love sincere and unadulterated.

## Translator's Notes

- The requested books are Galenic, including commentaries on Hippocrates and perhaps Galen's work on Hippocrates and Plato.
- The agricultural language draws on the parable of the sower.
