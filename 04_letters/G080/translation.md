## English Translation

The Lord has passed over your sin - or rather, things that were not nearly so great as they always stood before your eyes. I admire this too in you, my most august son: that you see even the gnats of sins as camels, contrary to the reproach addressed by the divine tongue to the Pharisees, and that you have reckoned the speck in the eye as a heavy beam. Our discourse sets this down as a great foundation of virtue, since we believe self-condemnation to be the beginning of salvation.

So may you go on in this way, if perhaps you might become a light for mortals. Let nothing belonging to the world, by which the lighter sort of us are moved about like pieces on a board, displace you from goodwill. Be unshakably persuaded of this: in general, it is a shame for Christians to have to be legislated into choosing not to hurt anyone. This is a commandment even of nature, which the disciple of the Gospel is ordered to surpass. The law restores the human being from what is against nature to nature; the word of God sets those who obey it even above nature. This, indeed, is the achievement and victory of his union with nature.

Still, because the savagery of the many rages against their own kind, the better sort of people are now made to be asked for this as a great contribution - I mean the wish to cause pain to no one. But we ask you to deliberate steadily for the good of all, and in this way to keep the unity of the faith in the bond of love. To bite and devour one another belongs neither to nature at all nor, surely, to faith. In this way you will draw more richly to yourself the graces of the good God, by whom may you be kept above every corruption.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter plays on Matthew 23:24 and Matthew 7:3-5.
- The final phrase is broken in the OCR but the sense continues Theophylact's standard closing wish.
