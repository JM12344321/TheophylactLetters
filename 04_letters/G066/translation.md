## English Translation

Your Theophylact is not so without love of beauty, holy master and father, nor sprung from oak or pine, as not to be overcome by the spiritual graces of holy Gregory, and before these by the dignity that encloses him. I too was captured by the man's Siren songs, and admired the composure of his character, which passes even into externals and gives grace to his outward habit.

For these reasons I have so far refused none of his requests, and was eager to hand over my whole self to his further wishes. What, then, has happened to me? Consider whether I am not rather being more provident than more timid.

Your holiness knows to what a great person Gregory is subject, and how necessarily that person needs his skill in ecclesiastical service, as a minister in sacred melody and as a leader among singers. Let this be my first consideration. The second, and especially important, is that you know better than anyone that it is not without danger to receive people transferred from monasteries or other churches without the opinion of those who preside over them.

How, then, could I have kept this man, whom I admire and love, in my own church, when he had received no release from the person holding the monastery and did not even have a letter from the hegoumenos providing you with freedom from trouble? You know, if anyone does, the men who throw everything into confusion: the spies upon bishops. For the time being they overlook the canons lying in a corner, full of dust and mildew, or they throw them in the middle and trample them arrogantly. But whenever some episcopal action must be done, then they untie the canons, curl and perfume them, and set them against us as offspring of the Spirit, bitterly proving the transgression.

For these reasons, then, I did not keep the man with myself. I advised him that he ought to receive a written release from his superiors and from the hegoumenos something like a document of dismissal. Since now, as I have learned from his own letter, he is not released in writing by those who lead the monastery, I do not know what I shall do. I long beyond words to attach him to the church set under our care, but I fear envy and human malice, which can fan into flame even a settled anger among those able to do evil.

Still, if it seems sufficient to your holiness to remove us from condemnation that he bring to us a letter with only the hegoumenos' signature, assigning him to us as is fitting, let him come bringing it, and he will be received by us gladly. But if you too do not see even this as sufficient, and if the release of those above him is absolutely necessary, I shall grieve at being deprived of such a man, one rare among eunuchs for good character and simplicity. I shall count this also as an addition to my other injuries and entrust the resolution of the matter, as is fitting, to the one able to bear all that causes grief. By him may we be kept above the scandals of this age through your holy prayers.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter concerns the possible transfer or reception of Gregory, a musically skilled eunuch/cleric attached to a monastery or church.
- The sharp aside about people weaponizing the canons is translated closely because it matters for Theophylact's ecclesiastical self-defense.
