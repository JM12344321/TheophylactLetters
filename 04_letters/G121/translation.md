## English Translation

It is possible both to write and not love, and not to write and love, most honored brother and master. The first uses the leaven of the Pharisees, which we have been taught is hypocrisy; the second is dragged around by occupations, which the great power of the life set against us brings upon lighter men such as ourselves.

Do not, then, count my not writing as a charge, but enjoy the greater good of love. It is right to consider the lesser thing worthy of lesser concern, and to stretch out the hand of prayers to those in danger of being submerged from every side. As though the other burdens were not enough for the weakness of my soul, my brother's death has been added: a brother dependent on my very breath, who was everything to me and, as they say, would throw himself into fire and over swords so that I might live in ease and freedom from pain. Deprived of him, I could not state in words how much help I need from elsewhere.

Let the greater part of that help, then, be your prayers, honored sir, given always in greater measure than usual. Perhaps they will be given only for a little while. Perhaps we shall follow our most kind brother on the same journey. I perceive my body flowing down into complete weakness and about to suffer what the stream joined to it naturally works upon it - unless, at that moment especially, we shall need the help just mentioned, so that the robber on the road may not find in us many things, nor very great ones, by which he adds people like me to his own portion. Rather, may we be found servants of the Master who cannot be taken, in whom that chief robber found nothing. By him may we be guided without stumbling toward our departure, as the Father's right hand slaps away all those who wish to drag us back.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter mourns the death of Theophylact's brother and asks for intensified prayers.
- "The robber on the road" and "found nothing in him" evoke death/the devil and John 14:30.
