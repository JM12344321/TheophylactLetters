## English Translation

I had already become dead, most august and greatest helper of mine, and had been set in the lowest places of Hades. Now I am raised by you. Restoring my little soul, you gather my bones, fit together what had fallen apart, grow flesh over it, stretch skin around it, and become for me a giver of life.

Do you wish me to draw aside the curtain of obscurity and show you what my words mean? When I looked at the confusion of the present life, the outrage of greed, and the shamelessness that had prostituted itself so brazenly and polluted affairs; when I saw those who handle public business behaving more like plunderers than tax-collectors, and treating divine laws and imperial commands alike as spider webs - Anacharsis' saying to Solon, that they hold flies but are torn by wasps; when I saw the goods of the Christian people carried this way and that, with no one redeeming or saving them - every hope of salvation had been stripped away. One would not be wrong to call that hope the breath of a soul fixed in calamities. All my reasonings were dissolved, and I lay dead in despair of any correction of the evil.

But when your most noble son-in-law, the shoot of goodwill, the lily in the valleys of this present flood of evils, the rose among the thorns that prick everyone, a truly God-breathed soul, came over the affairs near us, he appeared to me as a strange sight: a sweet river flowing through brine, a traveler passing through mud without being stained. I recognized, surely, the effects of your commands. Then the winged breath of better hope flew back into me, and everything took place around me that belongs to Ezekiel's fearful vision.

Do I owe you moderate thanks in prayers and praises, you giver of soul and giver of life? Or do I owe as many thanks - if it is lawful to say it - as curses to the incorrigible enemies of goodness, those destroyers of souls and unmakers of life?

But what have we suffered! The young man, truly a lily and a rose, gave fragrance for a little while. Rather, he became fragrant to God through the poor, God smelling the scent of his works more sweetly than the sacrifice of Noah of old. Then he passed over into your air, leaving us only the memory, without consolation. Who from now on will gather abundance for those who own no land, and lead those submerged in the drink of poverty into the order of plenty? Who will give orphans almost reason to confess gratitude to death, because by being deprived of weak fathers they gained the man who then appeared to them? Whose commands, echoes of divine goodness, will set a boundary firm for widows?

Whose eyes, shedding tears for the wronged, will mourn still more for those who do wrong, since they are diminished in greater things, and will hasten to undo the losses for both sides - the greater loss for one, the lesser for the other? Whom will the poor find as avenger against rapacious rich men, and the rich as advocate against malicious and slanderous poor men? Whom will the order of God's servants find as an attendant who does not make discriminations among ranks, the thing pursued by the godless, who in dishonoring God's servants furnish themselves with an apology against those entitled to honor by alleging their unworthiness?

Rather, he looked at all of them as better than himself; he believed that through them he was sanctified by the anointing of the Spirit, and that divine and sanctifying rites were still being performed. Those who did not think this way he considered uninitiated, incomplete, and without any share in sanctifying grace. For those who believe that the priest is a sanctifier, and know that they themselves are sanctified by him, are impious if they dishonor the sanctifier; if they do not think him such, they show themselves deprived of sanctification according to Christ. The one who is sanctified is sanctified by a sanctifier. Why must such people push their way into Christian affairs at all?

Who could bear even to see, much less to hear, an unbeliever set in so great a height over necessities, lest the Bulgarians even murmur? In one respect only did that most august man cause pain: to those who served him genuinely. They were not merely deprived of profits but also spent what they had before, and, according to the proverb, found coals instead of treasure and were burned by the things from which they expected delight. Or rather, to speak more truly, he did not pain even them. They judged the young man's magnificence in such works to be greater than all profit, an acquisition of wealth and not a loss. This is the mark of good servants: to choose poverty that enriches their master with good report rather than wealth that impoverishes him through ill report.

But for that noble and good sebastos the place of office was something to flee, truly a place of corruption, a tent of Kedar, an iron furnace. For this reason he flies almost winged to your blessedness. Those who enjoyed his kindness lament themselves, since they will be tested by hardships more heavily than before - evil is worse after one has had experience of the good. Yet they will pray from the Lord every outpouring of good for you and yours, because you teach in this way and they are taught in this way. I am not ashamed to be ranked first among them. May God grant to you yourselves, to your household, and to those around you completion in the good, a good flow of prosperity, keeping you utterly above every corruption and injury.

## Translator's Notes

- The Ezekiel allusion is to the vision of the dry bones in Ezekiel 37.
- The letter praises Adrian through the just conduct of his son-in-law while criticizing oppressive fiscal officials.
