## English Translation

Alas, the reverent man has perished from the earth, and our lamp has set. Gone is the image of humility; gone is the statue of gentleness. The plant of goodness has lost its flower, and with it the plant of uprightness that makes one like God. Where is that careful quality of character, inclined toward what is hidden? Where the approachable tone of speech? Where the surpassing love of friends? Where the peaceable disposition toward enemies, the readiness to yield, lacking nothing of the magnetic stone?

But ah, what am I suffering! I try to lament the blessed man, to beat myself for the one who has reigned, to keep mourning on a feast. Will you not come to your senses, foolish man, and behold the great John standing bright with brightness beside his longed-for Jesus? Will you not be lifted up by what is seen and call the worthy man blessed?

For you, that lamp has set and leapt away from earth and earthly things; but for God he rises together with the sun that is so by nature, himself a sun by participation. You list his good qualities, but consider this too: heaven is worthy of them. Why mourn a mind that has gone to its own place? You might as well blame fire because it tends upward and never denies its own nature; you might as well mourn an eagle flying high near the clouds; or, when seeing gold and pearls restored from worthless, trampled places and from shells that were disregarded, tear out your hair.

Such things would be great folly. Place this too among his allotments. Do not torment yourself over his painful sojourn. Even if he had been delighted with life and everything had been well for him, still it would not have been right to show such heavy grief and love of tears. But if he regarded life as truly bitter death and prayed to escape from prison and have his bonds broken, would we not be doing the work of enemies if, when the cord of his pains has been cut, we ourselves wept and mourned inconsolably?

Do we beat ourselves because it was good for us to have such a man among us? First, this is self-love, if we place our own good before what was fitting for him. For to depart and be with Christ is better for him. Then the opposite is entirely true: now we shall obtain help from him more than before, insofar as he has drawn near to the only helper and can converse with him without mediation.

These things, my good child, whom I travailed with, begot, and weaned in the Lord, my reason has argued back against grief. I thought it necessary to take you too as a partner in the goods that have come to me from this reasoning, as has long been my habit.

Show that blessed man alive in yourself again and again. Since God has entrusted rule to you, consider his gentleness toward those subject to him, his sociability, his carefulness in all things, his good counsel, and his doing nothing without reflection. Above all and before all, consider his reverence toward God and fear of evil, his respectfulness and propriety toward human beings, and all the other things I observed in him and which you have by nature from him. For you have sprung from that root by the God who causes all good things both to spring up and to grow. Show branches rising straight from it, bearing fruit and gladdening both God and human beings. Only hold fast to the root and do not be torn away from the richness that comes from it.

## Translator's Notes

- This is a consolation letter after the death of "the great John"; it argues against grief by turning the deceased into an intercessory helper.
- The bird, fire, gold, and pearl comparisons all serve the same point: noble things naturally return upward or to honor.
