## English Translation

Now is the time for me to utter the prophetic cry, most honored brother: "Alas, for fire has consumed the beautiful things of the inhabited world!" What is more beautiful than a divine house? What is more delightful, what more comely than the beloved tabernacles of God and the place of his glory? Alas for my sins, which perhaps set fire to the sanctuary of God. Who will give water to my head and a fountain of tears to my eyes, so that I may weep both for the shattering of the daughter of my people - I mean this pitiable little city, Jonah's gourd, appearing and perishing at the same moment - and for what I count still more lamentable, the mother of my people, the Church of God?

What soul is so adamantine, what heart so hardened, as not to be burned itself, not only by the sight but even by the bare report? What is this, Lord? For I will speak to you in a question. How have you thrust us away and cast us down? You have shown hard things not only to the people but also to the priests. You have made us drink the wine of compunction, left your house desolate, silenced the sound of singers from it; everyone who passes by will hiss at it. You have dishonored the diadem of beauty and torn down the crown of glory and praise. How is this, Lord? How have you chastened us not with judgment but in anger? How has your wrath acted so abruptly, and so strangely toward your own, exacting vengeance and bringing all to an end?

Long ago you overthrew Elijah's altar with heavenly fire, a fire that licked up even the dust, in order to glorify the righteous man. Now you have handed over your holy altar to fire, not for our glory but for our dejection. We have sinned, but you are longsuffering. We have done wrong, but you repent over human evils; you pass over iniquities and overlook sins. How, then, have you emptied upon us the whole dregs of unmixed gall?

But ah! See what I have suffered, swept along by the event. I have gone out of myself, and I have treated the visitation as an inconsolable disaster. Yet things are not so, not at all: the one who chastened is a Father, and those chastened are sons.

Rise, then, most holy brother. When else will you show the steadiness of mind that belongs to you, and the greatness of soul that marks you? If we ourselves lose heart so and are softened by what has befallen us, when shall we heal the pain of others? How shall we anoint them for courage if we ourselves have fallen beyond recovery? How shall we raise others from their fall?

Do not be in such a state for me, minister of the mighty God, you who ought to be strong in all things through Christ who strengthens you, and able, according to your teacher Paul, to support the weak. It would have been good if what happened had not happened. Who will deny it? But since it seemed good to God, who has resisted his will? Perhaps what happened is even better. We must not look for ways to feed grief - grief grows fat of itself and needs no belly-filling persuader - but for ways to tear away even the grief already present and make it wither. So too in bodily illnesses: beating the breast, cutting oneself, and tearing oneself belong to women; restraining these things and devising a cure for the sickness belongs to the physician.

But the cure of the suffering is difficult, especially in times so hostile to beauty and in days that bring every compulsion of works. Yet where is the power of the one who established all things by his word? He will speak and they will come to be; he will command and they will be created.

I will remind you of an old and divine history. That famous temple, the great marvel of the inhabited world, which Solomon built for God in Jerusalem, was burned and destroyed by Nebuzaradan. Then, when the people had been released from captivity, God stirred all toward rebuilding the divine house. Human weakness, measuring itself, did not dare the work, as though it were impossible to finish. So Haggai the prophet, moved by God, shamed the faint-hearted, broke the weakness of unbelief, and promised greater and more splendid things: "The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former," says the Lord almighty.

Think that these same words are now being sounded into your ears by the same Spirit. Let them be dew from God and a cure for your great despondency. How often you wished to see a finer and more delightful church! Then one thing and another intervened and cooled your ardor for the work. What do you know, then, if the wise steward of our affairs has so arranged yours that choice has become necessity, and the work no longer a mere embellishment but a making anew? The one who contrived this will also make the hard things easy, and the impossible possible. Is he wise but not powerful? Powerful but not good? All these things, but less concerned for his own glory? No, no. As wise, he foresaw what was better; as good, he did not neglect what is ours; and as good he will accomplish the good and profitable thing he foresaw, and, as one who wishes to be glorified, he will do it swiftly.

Apart from these things, he has set before you the suffering that has occurred as a test, so that you may show eagerness for its healing and bear the rest as far as strength allows. He knows that we often do not undertake good things of our own accord; for this reason he also drives us unwillingly toward good action. This is what I understood in the Gospels when some are compelled to enter the banquet. O sweet tyranny! O violence that sets free! O Father, kissing as he bites! O bowels of mercy burning as they burn, and for that very reason at once pouring forth abundant dew!

Let us not further provoke him by wailing against the scourge, as though that were a good thing, nor call down a second and perhaps heavier blow against the lightest one by behaving like murmurers. Rather let us show that he has dealt with us as sons, by approaching him as Father. We would approach him so if we slapped grief away as the offspring of another father, the father of darkness and confusion, and gave him the hands of our hopes, seeking from him his aid and support.

These words are from us to you, minister of the Word and our brother. You may purchase more from yourself, words by which you may charm the suffering and become a friend both to yourself and to others through the consolation of the Paraclete.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter laments a church fire and turns quickly from prophetic lament to pastoral exhortation.
- The end of the PG packet is slightly clipped after the reference to the Paraclete; the translation preserves the recoverable sense.
