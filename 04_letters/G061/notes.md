## Commentary

- Numbering: PG/Meursius prints this as `ΕΠΙΣΤΟΛΗ ΠΡΩΤΗ`; it is filed here as Gautier `G061` on Mullett's concordance/citation of Meursius I as G61.
- Address: `πανσέβαστέ μου ἀντιλῆπτορ` is rendered "my most venerable protector." Mullett refers to this letter as addressed to `?John Komnenos`; the question mark and exact social/political setting must be checked against Gautier.
- `ἔτι τι καὶ χολῆς ὄφελος`: proverbial consolation, rendered "there is some use even in bile." The point is that slander has accidentally produced the benefit of a letter from the addressee.
- Daniel allusion: "God has remembered me" evokes Daniel in the lions' den in the Greek Daniel/Bel and the Dragon tradition; Theophylact sharpens it with the comic phrase "lions that were not fasting."
- Abraham allusion: the phrase about not destroying the righteous with the impious refers to Genesis 18:23-25.
- `Μαργίτης`: the proverbial fool of the pseudo-Homeric *Margites*. The translation keeps the name because it functions as a learned insult.
- Lycurgus: the Homeric quotation points to *Iliad* 6.130-131 and the story of Lycurgus, son of Dryas, who contended with the gods. The exact Greek line must be checked, since the OCR is damaged.
- Water/torrent imagery: Theophylact turns hostile "streams" of slander into purifying water.
- `τῷ ῥήματι τῆς δυνάμεως`: biblical phrasing, cf. Hebrews 1:3. The tone is ironic: he repeats what detractors apparently claim about his influence.
- "I gave my back to scourges and my cheeks to blows": Isaiah 50:6.
- "I will go down and see": Genesis 18:21, used to praise the addressee for investigation before judgment.

## Apparatus and Source Problems

- The current Greek text is reconstructed from PG/Calfa OCR. It is not a substitute for Gautier's edition.
- `τὸ [τῷ?] ἀπαρρησιάστῳ`: PG/OCR is unclear. Translation treats the sense as "through lack of freedom to speak."
- The clause beginning `Ἀλλὰ γὰρ ἐγὼ τοῖς τὰ τοῦ δημοσίου...` is semantically clear in outline but corrupt in detail. It likely concerns officials or fiscal/public business, but the exact administrative nuance needs Gautier.
- The OCR around `ὅστε καὶ ἄστρα σημαίνεσθαι` and the following verse-like line is poor; translation is provisional.
- `περὶ τὰ τῆς λίμνης`: translated as "concerning the lake." The dispute or property right should be identified from Gautier/Mullett if possible.
- `κακεντρέχειαν?`: the OCR suggests malicious interference or spiteful busying; keep provisional until collation.
- The final clause after `Καταβὰς, φησίν, ὄψομαι` is too damaged for a confident Greek reconstruction. Translation supplies only the probable blessing sense.
