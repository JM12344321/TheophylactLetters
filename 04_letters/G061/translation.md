## English Translation

I even acknowledge a debt of gratitude to the man who slanders me, my most venerable protector, because he has become the means by which I received a letter from your magnificence. So that old proverb was true after all: there is some use even in bile. For when I took the letter into my hands, I spoke Daniel's words in the pit, among lions that were not fasting: "God has remembered me." So great did I judge the divine gift I had received to be.

Nor was I clothed in the garment of gladness only because you had addressed me, but also because you gave me permission to address you in return, and opened my mouth. Before this it had been shut and speechless, as if bound by some chain, through want of freedom to speak.

But, you may say, I am in the way of those who handle public business. I admit that I have some connection with the public sphere, though I have come to fear it so greatly that even the stars are made into omens. For it seizes one thing after another, until a man is blamed, and then not blamed. And the thing that Abraham somewhere forbids God to do, it destroys the righteous together with the impious, whenever those who act are not illuminated by the spirit of discernment.

But even if I was an impediment to others too, I am not so much a Margites that I would pit myself against equals. "For not even mighty Lycurgus, son of Dryas, lasted long," and the rest of the epic line. Let the younger scholars string it together.

Yet this too seems to belong to the winter that has passed, during which torrents of slander poured down upon me. Still, the Lord is among us, and that unreal water will not overwhelm us. Our soul will pass through the torrent unharmed; and we shall wash ourselves, or rather by this water we shall be laundered clean, appearing pure from the very things by which people had hoped we would be defiled.

As for the other matters, my fellow bishop will report what he must: my just case concerning the lake, and the malicious interference around it; and how here I am supposed to be a very great man, mighty in strength, carrying all things by the word of power. Yet I lie exposed to everyone for insult and contempt; I became deaf, I was humbled, I kept silent, bereft of every good comforter, and I gave my back to scourges and my cheeks to blows.

A mouth formed by nature for slander no one could shut. But may it belong to your loftiness, raised above the cawing of jackdaws and the twittering of starlings, not to receive such sounds. And may you have this too from God: to say and do everything only after it has been searched out with judgment. For he, knowing all things before they come into being, says, "I will go down and see." [The closing blessing is corrupt in the PG/OCR text.] May he keep you, in likeness to himself so far as this is possible, above every evil mind and every injury.
