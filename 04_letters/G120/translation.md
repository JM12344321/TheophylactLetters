## English Translation

What shall I repay the Lord for all that he has repaid me? He led me through the wet so safely that I was safer than on dry land. He commanded the storm, and it stood as a breeze. He rescued me from a sickness of many days and girded me with strength, so that present things became equal to those of old - I mean the things around Hezekiah and the things seen by Ezekiel, the beholder of great visions, by the river Chebar, when a spirit of life and rebirth was renewed in dead bones. I need not mention the deeds of my John: Peter's mother-in-law, the royal son, the dropsical man, the paralytic, cooled from fever, restored from bad habit of body, joined together from paralysis.

Thanks, then, to the one who saves freely and does not act according to our iniquities, but overlooks sins and passes over accusations. When I boarded the ship, my most brilliant lord - it was the day on which my periodic attack was expected - something happened beyond all hope: seasickness, salivation, and vomiting as its consequence; and the fluid expelled was raw and unsettled. At the end came a portion of bile. After a short interval, the same things happened again, except that the bile was greater and bit more sharply in its passage.

I expected the sign of the quartan fever to occur in the usual way. But apparently the fever was spat out together with the humor and bile, and I was shown to have been fearing a fear that was no fear. Again came the day of the period, and I, cowardly in all things, lost hope; but God was the same and had not changed. The evil vanished, and its place was not found. Now I have been saved for you at Thessalonica, released from a heavy sickness. This, it seems, is the wise point of the proverb: there is some benefit even in bile. Perhaps in the time after this I shall give as a parody that a wave grants calm, brine grants peace, and the body is strengthened by injuries of the stomach.

So much for that. The affairs around Ohrid are all full of fear. The region of Diokros, a part of Ohrid, has been seized by the slave and apostate; Bagorrara - a very great place, lying between the Bulgarian and Dyrrachian mountains - is being guarded by the rebel. To speak generally, evil is badly established everywhere. Yet we take courage in God and in the efforts of the mighty emperor. Since this little breeze has at least been warmed by the rays of the emperor's benefactions, and for that reason the rebel lifts up his head, there will surely be a place where he sinks down.

The all-venerable sebastos and protostrator, lord Michael, has been sent by the emperor to collect men and oppose the accursed creature. He is already collecting them; he will attack him and invade his territory. The God who defends us will come and will not delay; and the slave Jeroboam, who attempted rebellion in the days of Solomon, will do nothing more than plan badly. Though he longs for kingship - O earth and sun! - and for the rank of kingship and incomparable height of rule, he will not even be judged worthy to be the slave of a king.

As for me, as soon as I reached Thessalonica, if I had found some Daedalus I would have fitted wings to myself, flown, and come to rest at Ohrid. But I had neither Daedalus nor any friend who could contribute to my need. So I have hired animals and am already on my way.

## Translator's Notes

- The first half is a medical/travel thanksgiving; the second reports a rebellion or armed seizure around Ohrid.
- Diokros and Bagorrara are provisional transliterations from noisy OCR.
