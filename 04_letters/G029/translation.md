## English Translation

"Am I some god to you, that you liken me to the immortals?" Yet friendship allows you even to tell a falsehood, though you have practiced the truth in the answers of Themis, whose champion you are.

For us it is enough to remain in the rank where we once stood: appointed as commanders of ten, or, thanks to you, commanders of thirty. The glory of command over a thousand and of generalship we only hear about. Do not hang the judgments of Longinus around my neck, lest you yourself seem to some people not to judge according to Longinus.

For we now fall short of our own former power, in which we were once strong, by as much as we then fell short of the condition of the ancients. These districts are unwilling to teach me; and books have been snatched away by Harpies - the hands of tax officials, depriving us of all reading no less than those creatures deprived Phineus of his food.

You, then, pass from walking to the Academy and proceed to the Stoa, and traverse its beauties. Feast for my sake also in the Ionian meadows, and through them receive Zephyrs in a relaxed breath. Pluck for me some of the roses of the Iliad, as many as are available to you both through these things and by your own wish. Sit as a spectator of tragedy; play with comic buffoonery; do not reject the pastoral pipe either, which makes springs, whispers of pine, and various labors into melody. Even if its erotic element seems contemptible to your Muses, do not reject it.

You, then, nourish yourself with these things and with what goes with them - or rather before them - with the bread that strengthens the heart. He came down from heaven and nourished those of old who walked through the wilderness in the training of evils; he now still promises and is able to nourish. Search out for me the Mosaic tablets, taking what is outward from them and investigating what is within. Search also the prophetic visions and likenesses, imagining them as far as possible, and being led by history as a guide toward imitation of the ancients.

Still more, look upon the Sun of grace and enjoy his rays as far as possible. Keep company with the Gregories and Basils and with the blessed choir around them. Feast yourself on all these good things; and pray with us that I may not lose even the mere sound of the lyre. Matters have gone so badly. But may I see your longed-for face and hear that speech of yours which I always admire before everything else, you who outshine all for me in words and deeds.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter is a witty response to literary praise, moving from Longinus and classical genres to Scripture and the Fathers.
- The Harpies/Phineus image complains that tax officials have stripped Theophylact of books and leisure.
