## English Translation

What then? Am I not going to forgive one who has so found out his sin and hated it so much that he made it public through a speech that justifies him? If the truth must be said, this is not even a matter of forgiving. No accusation preceded it; rather, we would set this too among the proofs of your good sense, because in your opening words you became your own accuser. As for me, I did not make that long silence of yours a charge against you, but a sign of my own sins. Because of them, among friends and neighbors some have stood against me, and I have said with Job: "My closest friends did not acknowledge me; those who knew my name forgot me; the Lord has done this to me and has used dreadful anger against me."

But since you have opened to us the gates of a friendly and comforting mouth, do not close them again by dozing off. If you do, then you would more readily and more justly be accused - not only because of the expectation you planted in the letter asking pardon, but because you know that no small penalty comes upon you from transgression if you do not mean to besiege me with your small inquiries and distinctions, by which you, who plume yourselves on Latin discourse, lead us more rustic men away.

Therefore you will be accused not only because you promised to write and then did not write, but also because you are not unaware that now, if ever, we need the speech of friends no less than those sick with a hard-to-heal disease need some Asclepius or Paean. Do not tell me about the medicines I have here, or that, having the boxes of them in my own possession, I may without trouble choose the suitable one, mix it, spread it on, apply it as a plaster, and fight the illness. We do not find even bodily physicians sufficient for their own diseases. They do the same thing as eyes: seeing the things of others, but needing others in order to see themselves.

Write, then, as often as you can; cool what is inflamed in me. Suggest methods of treatment, and help the sick man from every side. In this way you will both accomplish what is pleasing to the Word, to whom you have dedicated yourself, and be found following yourself: honoring those whom you say are honored by you and judging them worthy of your consolation - if of much and great consolation, because they are worthy of such things; if of little and small, because those who are truly worthy do not reject even such things.
