## English Translation

What is this? Has good John too been carried off to wrestle with Laestrygonians and Cyclopes? For that is how I judge the affairs that are called blessed among you. But so that you may not appear to be that much-devising son of Laertes, and so that you may have reason as the opponent of evil, Hermes, giving you the moly, has surely already explained everything well to you.

Do not worry, then. Use its power, and you will sail back safely to your own people with your ship still possessing its wits, and with a wealth more honorable: frankness before God, which virtue toward those you govern grants you. For now, believe that you are being tested no less than those who go down to the Olympic games. May it be granted me to hear that my companion is an Olympic victor. Perhaps I may even sing victory-songs for you, not at all less honorable than those of Bacchylides and Simonides, perhaps even fitted to the Boeotian lyre, and certainly [...]

## Translator's Notes

- This short item is embedded at the end of PG029 after the Pakourianos letter. The last line is corrupt in the OCR and probably clipped.
- The Homeric imagery is from Odysseus' encounters and Hermes' moly in Odyssey 10.
