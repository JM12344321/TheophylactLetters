## English Translation

Your holiness' letter was read, most honored brother, to all of us assembled brethren. We rejoiced greatly at the conversion of the Armenians, for having learned that there is joy in heaven even over one sinner who turns back, how could we not rejoice over the salvation of so many souls? We prayed that they may also have a steadfast establishment in the good, so that they may not be like plants violently pulled up by the hand and then return again to themselves.

But at your excusing yourself from the journey here, our hearts were wounded beyond measure, because your absence became the cause of suspending once again the matters still pending. Your written opinion seemed to us sufficient to make up for the deficiency caused by your absence. Some who examined it more carefully said that it was not straight or unambiguous, but strangling and crooked, far removed from the straight Spirit and suited rather to the coils of the crooked dragon, against whom the greatest-voiced of the prophets foretold that the holy sword of God would be laid; and that it was also composed as an insult to the synod.

For you say, "If you arrange anything not contrary to the divine canons and to the truth, I too follow this pious opinion." As it seems, then, the piety of your perfection has such an opinion of us as though we were manipulating the canons, opposing the truth, and setting forth impious opinions. If that is so, why should you tread the court of the Lord with us or enter the divine altar? Surely you too have as much boldness as David, perhaps even more, to say with great confidence: "I have hated the assembly of evildoers; I will not sit with the impious, nor will I enter with lawless men."

We do know that opinions may be given with qualifications. But even in such qualifications the proper respect for brothers is preserved, and those who compose opinions do not so carelessly and disdainfully sharpen their tongue like a serpent. Some, then, saw the greatest crookedness and an insult in your opinion. All of us, however, declared it simply invalid and uncertain, because it did not have the guarantee of your own handwritten signature.

Some of the sharper men also remarked this about your honor's letter: that without noticing it you had fallen into a very great contradiction. Above, you said that a visit here was impossible for you because you were about to go up to the City with the Armenians; but lower down you said that you were waiting also for the registrar of the theme, so that you would not leave affairs in that condition. If, because you were about to bring the Armenians before our mighty and holy emperor and display them to him, you excused yourself from the assembly requested not only by the canons but also by other causes known to you, then surely you will not wait for the registrar. But if you intend to spend time there because of him, so that no financial injury may happen to affairs from an accounting conducted in your absence, much more ought you to have spent the time in assembly with us and in the administration of ecclesiastical matters, where the benefit is more divine and the harm more dangerous.

Nothing so trips itself up as a mouth that has learned to say what is not, and tries to cure one falsehood by another. Since, then, we have determined, brother, that you deliberately postponed the assembly and sought and invented excuses - for that saying of Solomon is truly accurate, that a man who wishes to separate from friends seeks excuses - and since your opinion makes these things clear, composed as it was, unless some malice stood between, simply and with no artifice, I impose on you, in the Holy Spirit, the penalty of suspension from liturgy. You have become debtor to all for completing the judgment begun in the assembly.

For you must necessarily choose one of two things: either you did not then begin it according to the Spirit, or now you are withdrawing from it unspiritually. This too is surely clear: if those who turned toward Orthodoxy have indeed turned, you have by now performed the customary rites for them. If you have already performed them, you should have persuaded them to endure this harsh interval until your coming to us and your release from us. Those who were persuaded by you to accept the mystery, if indeed they were persuaded, would quickly have yielded to you in this as well. This too, if it had happened, would have been a test of the genuineness of the gift, and would have stopped the mouths of many who can say that the Armenians did not make their approach because they recognized the truth, as you openly declared. Recognition of the truth, among its other effects, disposes those who recognize it to endure.

Nevertheless, we yield this point too to you and allow you, after receipt of this letter, to be free for fifteen days. During all these days those who have come over will presumably be chrismated. After that you will have the yoke of suspension from liturgy laid upon your neck. If it seems harsh to you that the church under you should be deprived of your hierarchical ministry, consider that it is harsh for other churches too, because of your absence, to be deprived of bishops. Know well that if you yourself are not present, neither will the judgment concerning Lipenion receive its completion, nor will the other widowed churches receive bishops. Otherwise the tongue that strikes in secret will again have room to send cowardly and ignoble slanders against opponents, and to shoot in the dark at the upright in heart.

Since one of the Bulgarian monks, while we were sitting in council, also lamented before us about an old man, his brother, a monk and superior of the monastery in the Kastron of Saint John, saying that he has been confined and enjoys no consolation whatever from anyone, because your reverence has forbidden everyone by proclamation even to approach this monk at all - if these things are true, they are certainly unworthy of hierarchical humanity and necessarily worthy of swift correction. Let correction proceed from your honor, while the Lord keeps you showing forth in all goodness and truth the fruit of righteousness of the Spirit.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter is explicitly synodal, not merely private: "from the synod" is part of the heading.
- "Armenians" refers to a group being received into Orthodoxy; the exact community and rite should be checked against Gautier's apparatus.
