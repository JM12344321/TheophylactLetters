## English Translation

Who am I, my lord, lord, that you have loved me so far that I may now utter to you what David once uttered to God? "Why have you laid so great a burden on me, when all my strength for carrying burdens is broken?" You command me, as though I were the very pillars that hold up the broad heaven and as though I needed the strength of Atlas, to part the clouds of the prophetic writing and show the sun bare.

But who will blow away for me the cloud of affairs which the reports of slanderers have thickened over our honored sun? Whoever this sun may be, he is beautiful as a bridegroom, huge as a giant, giving life to all by his heat and warmth and conquering all. He ought to melt these clouds, so that even if they arise they do not appear; yet he thickens them into a formation of clouds - perhaps reasonably, but, as it seems to most, contrary to reason. Before we have clear weather from the former things, Eurus and Notus have fallen together again, and a bitter Zephyr, and night rushes down from heaven. This drives more and sharper goads of despondency into me, whenever I have darkness from above, from the place where I had hoped for light.

But lest I too make clouds by obscurity and shade the sun of speech, here is the matter. The unjust witnesses who rose against me yesterday and the day before - whom our mighty emperor, your much-loved brother, exposed in their falsehood and made fall from their plots, so that injustice itself was proved false - have now come to him again, saying the same things about the same matters. They have gained such power that they persuaded him to throw into the void those white votes which he had granted us as just, and to bring out black ones in their place, full of much gloom. That truly imperial judgment has been made to look like Penelope's web, woven by the day of truth and undone by the night of falsehood.

Most dreadful of all, we are being condemned in absence. As I have learned, the registrar sent against us has been ordered to remove from the Church the estate in Ohrid - an estate for which documents of exchange had already been issued, the Church having received it in place of the wandering properties which she ceded to the public treasury because of paroikic service. The matter was more necessary than everything else, and a chrysobull was added to establish the exchange. An imperial note had even threatened those Cercopes when they were pestering Heracles.

In these circumstances, what kind of soul do you think I have? How could I fail, with the divine Jeremiah, to feel pain in my belly and to have my senses rage, thrown into confusion from every side and almost driven to frenzy? The noble Thucydides says that what is sudden, unexpected, and contrary to reason enslaves the mind. Even by yesterday's slanders, though I was not firmly rooted out, I was bent down simply because such a thing had been sown in the good soil of the imperial hearing like hemlock, aconite, or some other poisonous fig.

By those slanders, then, I was bent down in my misery so that I stooped to the ground and inclined downward, having no official support, struck by slaves and forced to bear it. O Christ, who reigns in the palaces, and who did not even when judged and slapped cease to bear mildly a blow from a slave: whom do you suppose would hesitate to lay hands on us when he sees our accusers passing by like demigods on mules, as people say, making a drunken tale of this, that they fabricated this and that against a high priest before the most pious emperors, escaped judgment, and then laughed over it?

Here too David's word has been fulfilled in me: those who sit in the gates babbled against me, and those who drink wine made songs about me. Surely they will tolerate me very well if I rebuke, correct, or exhort them, whether they belong to the people or to the ecclesiastical body, when they have seen someone acting worse than anyone after the previous reproofs. Because those subject to those words became savage, they surrounded me with words of hatred and fought me without cause. Over all this, not only did they pay no penalty, but they shine in wickedness as though with a trophy; fear is nowhere. They remain like sheep forever and complete their life in good things. It is not untimely to speak with long-suffering Job.

I used to hear of the Lernaean Hydra, which became twice itself at each cutting and gave trouble to noble Heracles when he raised only a sword against it. The hero would have been in danger - or the labor would certainly have been too much even for the hero - if he had not added good Iolaus as a fire-bearing comrade to burn the cuts. What does the myth mean? It seems to me that wickedness - for that is the Hydra - does not need a superficial cutting and punishment. We would make the wicked more wicked if they were punished so mildly, since they would think they had mocked justice and would from then on speak their wickedness more fearlessly and nakedly. Boldness is multiplied in evil people when they experience a penalty milder than the severe one they expected. Therefore the outgrowths of evil must not only be cut off; the springs of the blood that produces and feeds the beastly heads must also be dried up. If evil shoots are not cut even in this simple way, then, as the proverb says, it is a Lerna of evils.

Even at the former outrages my heart abandoned me - by which I mean the boiling of spirit I used against every malice, when I showed my tongue truly a pupil of lordly tongues. If I now receive the blows from the man presently making the register, who, as they say, has received a heavy scourge from imperial hands and been ordered to use Isaiah's words against us in alien fashion - for this is what those who hate the reprover in the gates and abhor a holy word have accomplished, as they threaten - then let Baruch stand by me here as secretary to Jeremiah while he laments, preserving my calamities as songs for the future.

After such stains, and so many of them, have been splashed upon us, do you think the mirror of my mind can receive divine images, when even the light of my eyes is no longer with me? This is both likely and reasonable. Nevertheless, since we have entrusted our affairs to God, to the one who reigns from him and through him, and to the goodness of your Greatness, we are confident that our hope will not be put to shame, that the endurance of the poor will not perish in the end, and that wickedness will not overpower wisdom, if one must obey what wisdom itself suggests.

When we have become a little easier, we shall take up the work commanded and send a taste to you, who are most critical in taste. If the taste seems like flower-wine, the offspring of that vine whose farmer is the heavenly Father, we shall press out the remaining clusters. But if it is sour wine that bites the taste, the kind with which Christ was mocked by his crucifiers when they killed him outside the vineyard and outside the meaning of the Scriptures, then we shall know that we are laboring in vain. For the present, then, let your kindness endure our weakness, until we are lightened a little and our slack hands and paralyzed knees grow strong, Christ strengthening us, he who is power and who keeps us above weakness of soul and body, that is, above corruption and injury.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter answers an exegetical request from Adrian but postpones fulfillment because of renewed slanders and property seizure.
- The estate at Ohrid and the chrysobull/exchange passage need legal collation in Gautier.
