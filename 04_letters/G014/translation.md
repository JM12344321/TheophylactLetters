## English Translation

Truly, for the spiritual person even worldly things become spiritual, and God becomes everything in everything for him. Look, then: even the gifts sent by you to our lowliness, most reverend master, though they are worldly, hint at something spiritual.

The perfume made from drops of the rose, sent in two glass vessels, admonished my hardness. It taught that, if we make body and soul pure and transparent as glass, we may make them vessels of the myrrh that was emptied out for our sake. You surely know the one who drips like a drop upon the earth. His fragrance is astringent, because it is saving; and the astringency is fragrant, because it is not lax. He has a yoke, but it is good; he has a burden, but it is light. Again, he leads into the bridal chamber, but by a narrow way. He commands rejoicing, but adds that it is to be done with trembling.

And the fragrant woods - surely they were not sent by your spiritual wisdom in vain and idly? We ourselves would be very idle if we did not think something spiritual here too. Since the pieces of wood are four, what else did they signify but the fourfold wood that worked our salvation for us? Paul boasts in it, and he wishes us who belong to Christ to crucify the flesh together with its passions and desires. You have therefore taught us plainly that we must crucify ourselves to all the pleasant and fragrant things of the world.

One might even say wittily that the things sent were sent against themselves. For through their number, as has been said, we are taught to be crucified to such harmful things - or rather, to bring everything to God and through such things to come to knowledge of him who planted such honor through fragrance even in other, dishonored pieces of wood.

I would have had more to write, as I considered how you were teaching my lowliness through the things sent. But what profit is it to me to understand these things and not do them? May it come about through your prayers that I both understand and do what is needed, and that I make my own life an odor of life, not of death, for the flock looking to me. Do not cease asking this on our behalf.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter allegorizes gifts of rose perfume and four fragrant wood pieces.
- Major allusions include Matthew 11:30, Matthew 7:14, Psalm 2:11, Galatians 5:24, and 2 Corinthians 2:16.
