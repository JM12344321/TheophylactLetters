## English Translation

I have kept silence. Shall I keep silence forever? I kept silence because, after draining to satiety the cup of these evil days and being drunk with gall, as Jeremiah says, I lay ill and insensible to every speech and greeting. But now, as time has somewhat breathed away the sleep-making vapor and the stupor has been dissolved by the heat of the divine Word, I lay aside silence, especially since I have such a minister for my letter to you.

How are you, you who are undefeated in soul and incomparable in courage? In the one you show the good temperament of your body; in the other, the harmony of the powers of your soul. You have a mind in no way inferior to hands, and hands that accomplish what even mind would despair of. You have made an apology on behalf of human nature alone, proving that it has not grown old or rotted before the generation of men, but that the vigor of its strength is still alive.

You are sufficient alike in times of peace and in wars, having neither the practical nor the martial capacity attainable by word and wonder - or, to speak more truly, showing that each is surpassed by the other. Where are you for me, you who are desired by me everywhere, reservoir of every good, wholly dexterous in all things? Are you setting nations in order and training the judgments of men, becoming a Prometheus and shaping human beings with a civilized and humane formation? Or are you sitting above companies of soldiers, appointing commanders, deepening battle lines, marking the moments for attacks, and showing the barbarians that the old Roman spirit has not died in you?

May you have affairs worthy of that spirit and accomplish things better than our hopes and prayers. If you also make this clear by letter, you will truly build for us a golden roof. May you be kept by the Lord above every corruption and every harm.

## Translator's Notes

- The letter praises Gregory in both civil and military registers; "Prometheus" is retained as Theophylact's classical allusion.
