## English Translation

"Ah," you might say, "a little letter from Achris, and from an archiepiscopal tongue no less! Formerly this was not common. Surely there must be some great cause for writing now. The archbishop would not be ignorant that for a man around whom a swarm of imperial business buzzes, letters from others are no less a nuisance than his own writing to others."

From where, then, comes this strange new thing, untimely for us? From where? From your skill in every good. Since God has granted you great power with the emperor, you do not use the divine gift left-handedly, but show yourself worthy of greater power by administering what has now been given to you in a manner worthy of God. Displaying your energy in the talents entrusted to you, you show that to you too belongs the word spoken by the just Master and distributor to the worthy: "Be over ten cities."

For to give, in the authority now present to you, a ruler to the region under us - this is so far removed from the iron race of the present days that it approaches Hesiod's golden race. The man has newly united things naturally distant from one another: weight of character and moderation. The one he preserves for those who act wickedly; the other he pours out upon the more decent in a sweet stream. With the one he blocks the measures of the unjust; with the other he sharpens the good toward more manifest kindness, heals what is broken, and calls back what has been rejected.

He is plainly going to make Achris appear an object of mercy, before long even enviable. How could setting such a man over us not call forth from God greater power for you with the emperor, so that, through your choice of proper men and your recommendation with the ruler, you may benefit greater cities and nations?

This loosened my tongue and persuaded me to draw your hearing for a little toward my speech. For when I saw so great a good, and so strange a one, coming among us through you, I judged that I must not fail to declare to your great eminence our perception of the gift, nor fail to make known in words what sort of thing we have obtained. Besides, when you yourself approve your own judgment and, like a father, gladly embrace a fine offspring, you will wing him still more by letters for the course he has chosen, so that the good man may be shown better still, and so that a Nireus may now be fashioned also into an Achilles.

If this Achilles were admonished by you to assign some greater reverence to Phoenix or to Nestor, he would in no way belie the hero. Through everything you yourself will show that those now known to us have an unmixed bond toward you [...]
