## English Translation

Understand me in a better way, all-holy brother and master, and do not let the chaff concern me when I possess the wheat. My remaining here seemed for the moment, even to me, to have been well and reasonably arranged. But human calculations are timid things. It would have been better for me to run back to the city, to ask for treatment for my own body there, and to place far away from my companions the experience of the burning heats here.

None of these good things is now enjoyed by them, and least of all by my brother, whom I still possess as sparks of consoling refreshment. Having exchanged the air in Ohrid for the air here, and being deprived of the light, thin wine there and of other healthier foods, his body is in a bad state and fares very poorly. He has reached the extreme of loss of appetite, struggles with headaches, and has acquired a stomach full of humors. Since the season allows us to use nothing else for cutting these, we use oxysaccharon, and even that sparingly, though we would use other things too if we had them.

But he is averse to every medical application. For these reasons I mourn and grow gloomy, honored man of God, and I blame my remaining here, judging in a smaller way than is right. For if it had seemed good to God that we be grieved, we would have been grieved wherever we were.

Nevertheless, make him propitious to us. He knows how to pass over iniquities and sink sins in the sea - whatever sea Micah knew this to be, whether the sea of baptism, repentance, or his mercies. It is this sea that gives the others their saving existence. If you pour it over us, you will save our weakness, drowning whatever Egyptian and enemy lies within us, both of sicknesses of soul and sicknesses of body. Do not be careless, then, but open the cataracts from above.

## Translator's Notes

- Oxysaccharon is a medicinal vinegar-sugar preparation.
- The sea that drowns sins alludes to Micah 7:19.
